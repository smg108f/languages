Czech is the language of a hilltop fortress a Slavic prince built above an existing river settlement around 880, one that would go on to serve as an actual Holy Roman Emperor's seat five centuries later and still dominates the city's skyline as a UNESCO-listed castle complex today. It got its earliest continuous chronicle written down around 1314, watched Protestant exiles produce a six-volume Bible translation so influential it single-handedly standardized the modern literary language, and nearly vanished from educated life by the late 18th century before a deliberate 19th-century revival dragged it back from the edge of extinction. It drinks more beer per capita than any other nation on the planet, invented the pale lager style the rest of the world just calls "pilsner," and turns into a genuine national fever every time its ice hockey team takes the ice, regardless of how much more football is actually played on the ground.

#### eras
---
Czech changes with whoever's controlling the Bohemian basin: an ancient Great Moravia era predating a distinct Czech identity, a Přemyslid Duchy/Kingdom of Bohemia era launched by Prague Castle's founding, a Habsburg era beginning with the Battle of White Mountain's suppression of the Bohemian nobility, an Austro-Hungarian crownland era, and a post-1918 Czechoslovak/Czech Republic era including the National Revival's completion and the 1993 split from Slovakia.
