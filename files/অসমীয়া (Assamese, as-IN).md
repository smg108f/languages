Assamese is the language of a Brahmaputra Valley kingdom founded by a prince who took thirteen years just to cross the mountains into it, then spent the next six centuries running an administration so effective it fought the Mughal Empire to a standstill at a river battle still taught as a masterclass in outnumbered naval tactics. It produced its first surviving literary work as a translation of an epic already thousands of years old, deliberately written to strip its hero of any actual heroism, and it now shares its script proudly with Bengali while insisting, quite reasonably, that everyone stop calling it a dialect of it. It brews its own rice beer at home for a spring harvest festival, wraps sticky rice and sesame into sweets for the same occasion, and quietly supplies a very large share of the world's tea from gardens whose ownership history traces back to one of its own 19th-century planters.

#### eras
---
Assamese changes with whoever's controlling the Brahmaputra Valley: an ancient Kamarupa era ruled from Pragjyotishpura, an Ahom Kingdom era launched by a Tai prince's founding of a fixed capital, an Ahom-Mughal conflict era decided at a decisive river battle, a British colonial era of tea-driven administrative reorganization, and a post-1947 Indian statehood era.
