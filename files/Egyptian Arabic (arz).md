Egyptian Arabic is the language of a country so confident in its own cultural gravity that it decided the entire Arab world should understand its street slang, and then made that happen through sheer volume of movies and pop songs rather than any government asking permission. It speaks a dialect nobody official ever writes down and everybody everywhere still somehow recognizes from the television, treats a decades-old outdoor coffeehouse as a more essential civic institution than most government buildings, considers a bowl of lentils, rice, and pasta drowned in tomato sauce and fried onions a legitimate national dish rather than a cry for help, and has spent a century exporting its own colloquialisms to two dozen countries that officially insist on speaking something more formal.

#### eras
---
Egyptian Arabic changes with whoever's ruling the Nile and how loudly Cairo's studios are broadcasting: an Islamic conquest that displaced Coptic as the country's everyday tongue within three centuries, a Muhammad Ali dynasty that modernized the state while colloquial Cairene speech spread through a growing capital, a 20th-century cinema and radio boom that made Cairo's dialect the default voice of pan-Arab entertainment, a revolutionary era that overthrew a monarchy and later a president in the language of the street rather than the palace, and a contemporary social-media era spreading Egyptian slang further and faster than the old film studios ever could.
