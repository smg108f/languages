Khmer is the language of an empire whose first king declared himself a god-on-earth on a mountaintop in 802, and whose successors, a century later, built a capital so architecturally ambitious that its central temple complex remains the largest religious monument on the planet. Its oldest surviving inscription was carved into stone in 611, nearly two centuries before that empire even existed, making its writing system one of the oldest continuously used scripts in Southeast Asia. Its 20th century produced a revolutionary government that emptied its own capital city at gunpoint and killed an estimated 1.5 to 2 million of its own citizens in under four years, a catastrophe that erased a generation of teachers, monks, and musicians the country is still rebuilding around. Its neighbor invaded to end that regime in 1979, only for the fighting to drag on for another decade before the country finally held UN-supervised elections and restored its monarchy. It didn't organize a formal sign language for its Deaf community until 1997, decades after most of the rest of the world. It steams fish curry inside a folded banana leaf, ferments fish into a pungent paste that flavors nearly everything else it cooks, and races long dragon boats down the Tonlé Sap every autumn to celebrate the one time of year the river famously reverses its own current.

#### eras
---
Khmer changes with whoever's controlling the Tonlé Sap basin: an ancient Angkor Empire era beginning with Jayavarman II's god-king proclamation, a post-Angkor/Longvek era following the 1431 sack of Angkor, an independence era after the end of French colonial rule, a Khmer Rouge/genocide era, and a post-1993 Constitutional Monarchy era.
