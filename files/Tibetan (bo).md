Tibetan is the language of an empire whose script was designed by a single minister sent on a seven-year expedition to India specifically to bring back an alphabet, an expedition so dangerous that he was reportedly the only member of his party to survive and return. Its capital was chosen by the same king who commissioned that alphabet, a fortress town on a river that grew into a walled city once two of his foreign-born wives arrived carrying Buddhist statues that needed temples built around them. Its national epic, an oral poem about a divine warrior-king, has been sung and expanded for roughly a thousand years by traveling bards and now runs longer than the Iliad, the Odyssey, the Mahabharata, and the Ramayana combined, without ever having been definitively finished. Its 20th century included a full military annexation in 1950 and a 1959 uprising that ended with its spiritual leader fleeing on foot in disguise across the Himalayas into India, and disputed estimates of the resulting death toll range from the low tens of thousands into the tens of thousands more. It didn't get an organized, standardized sign language until a formal project launched in 2001 to unify the country's many local varieties. It churns its tea with salted yak butter instead of milk, eats a roasted-barley staple that requires no cooking equipment at all, and still keeps well over a hundred living reciters of an epic no single person has ever heard start to finish.

#### eras
---
Tibetan changes with whoever's controlling the plateau: an ancient Tibetan Plateau settlement era predating recorded history, a Yarlung Dynasty/Early Kingdom era tied to the legendary first king, a Tibetan Empire era once Songtsen Gampo unifies the plateau, a Chinese Annexation era beginning in 1950, and a post-1959 Tibetan Diaspora era following the Dalai Lama's exile.
