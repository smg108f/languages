Catalan is the language of a county whose 9th-century count was legendary enough to have five bloody stripes on a golden shield attributed, almost certainly apocryphally, to his own dying fingers dragged across it, an image that became a flag still flown today. Its oldest surviving substantial text is a landowner's formal list of grievances against a local lord, recorded around 1105, making its first literary monument, technically, a lawsuit. A dynastic marriage in 1137 folded its home county into a kingdom twice its size, and the resulting Crown of Aragon lasted until a 14-month siege in 1713-1714 ended with 23,000 combined dead or wounded and Catalonia's own institutions abolished by the victor, a defeat its speakers now commemorate every September as a national holiday. It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1800, and that first one lasted barely two years before closing. It builds human towers up to ten people tall as a matter of civic pride, splits open a tomato and rubs it straight onto bread instead of dicing it, and toasts nearly everything with a sparkling wine grown specifically so it wouldn't have to import French champagne.

#### eras
---
Catalan changes with whoever's controlling the eastern Iberian coast: an ancient Hispania/Frankish Marches era predating the county's founding, a County of Barcelona era after Wilfred the Hairy's line takes hold, a Crown of Aragon era following the 1137 dynastic union, a Bourbon Centralization era once Catalonia's institutions are abolished, and a post-1939 Francoist Repression era.
