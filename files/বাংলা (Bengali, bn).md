Bengali is the language of a river delta so fertile and so densely populated that it fought and won a war partly on the principle that its script deserved a place on a national flag's honor roll, becoming the only language whose 1952 street protests over its own alphabet are now a UNESCO-recognized International Mother Language Day. It treats a single fish, prepared in mustard gravy, as a matter of regional pride bordering on scripture, considers a Nobel Prize in Literature a natural consequence of being spoken at all, and has kept two capital cities, two countries, and two currencies humming along in the same tongue without ever quite agreeing on how much fish sauce is appropriate at a given meal.

#### eras
---
Bengali changes with whoever controls the delta between the Ganges and Brahmaputra: an ancient Prakrit-descended era predating any fixed "Bengali" identity, a 7th-century Gauda kingdom that briefly unified the region under one crown, a Pala-dynasty golden age that gave rise to a distinct Bengali script, a 19th-century "Bengal Renaissance" that produced a Nobel laureate, a 1947 partition that split the delta along religious lines, a 1952 language movement and 1971 independence war that made Bengali the founding principle of an entire nation, and a present-day era spanning two countries and a vast global diaspora.
