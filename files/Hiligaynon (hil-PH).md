Hiligaynon is the language of a port city that spent one afternoon in 1898 as the last Spanish colonial capital anywhere on Earth, the morning after Spanish forces surrendered it to local revolutionaries in a plaza that has since been renamed for the liberty they won there. Its first novel was written in 1894 by a man who waited thirteen years to publish it, and its first proper municipal charter didn't arrive until 1937, unifying four separate riverside towns into the city that would eventually crown itself the "Queen City of the South." It didn't get a Deaf-education institution of its own, instead borrowing the same Manila school the rest of the archipelago leans on, founded three decades before independence. Its most famous soup was invented in a single district to use up spare noodles and offal, and every January it throws a festival, borrowed in spirit from a neighboring province's older one, that turns an entire city into a drumline in devotion to a Christ child brought over by a Spanish friar.

#### eras
---
Hiligaynon changes with whoever's controlling the Panay-Negros river deltas: an ancient pre-colonial Panay settlement era tied to the legendary Bornean-datu land purchase, a Spanish colonial era beginning with Arevalo's founding, a global-trade-port era after Iloilo's harbor opened to foreign shipping, a brief Philippine Revolution/last-Spanish-capital era, and a post-1946 Independent Philippine Republic era.
