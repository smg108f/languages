Maithili is the language of a kingdom so ancient that its own foundational epic casts its legendary king as a philosopher first and a ruler second, and whose capital city still stands today as a cross-border pilgrimage town that two modern nations both claim a piece of. It kept a distinct rounded script alive for roughly a thousand years mostly in the hands of Brahmin scribes before quietly ceding ground to Devanagari, produced in one 14th-century poet enough erotic devotional verse to reshape Bengali religious music for centuries afterward, and organized its own elite families into a centuries-old genealogical registry expressly so cousins wouldn't accidentally marry each other. It grows the overwhelming majority of the world's fox nuts, once housed British India's single richest landholding family, whose jewelry collection reportedly out-sparkled the Nizam of Hyderabad's, and still can't quite get its own small, earnest film industry a permanent home in either of the two countries it straddles.

#### eras
---
Maithili changes with whoever's controlling the ancient Videha heartland: an ancient Videha era ruled from the legendary court of King Janaka, a Magadha-conquest era that ended local political independence, a Tirhuta-script era of Brahmin-scribed manuscripts, a literary golden age launched by the poet Vidyapati, a Darbhanga Raj era of vast zamindari wealth, and a modern recognition era that finally gave the language official constitutional status in India.
