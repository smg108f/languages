Shona is the language of a kingdom whose capital was the largest stone structure built anywhere south of the Sahara before European colonization, a walled royal city with no mortar holding a single one of its granite blocks together, occupied for over two centuries before its own royal family split apart and founded a second empire further north along the Zambezi. Its writing system wasn't unified until 1931, when a colonial-era conference forced five competing missionary orthographies into a single alphabet still in use today. It fought a war of independence through the 1960s and 70s that killed up to 30,000 people, only for its new government to turn around within a few years and kill a similar number of its own ethnic-minority citizens in a five-year campaign it later called "a moment of madness." It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1947, run by Dominican missionary sisters in a mining town. It eats a stiff cornmeal porridge with almost every meal, snacks on fried mopane caterpillars for protein, and considers a strong sorghum beer, sold in a cardboard carton you shake before opening, one of its most reliably exported cultural products.

#### eras
---
Shona changes with whoever's controlling the Zimbabwean plateau's goldfields and cattle country: an ancient Bantu-settlement era predating any centralized state, a Great Zimbabwe Kingdom era, a post-Great Zimbabwe/Mutapa Empire era after the royal family split and moved north, a British colonial Rhodesia era, and a post-1980 Independent Zimbabwe era.
