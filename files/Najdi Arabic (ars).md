Najdi Arabic is the language of a landlocked plateau that spent most of history being the empty middle of everyone else's map and then produced the dynasty that named the whole country after itself. It speaks with the clipped confidence of a people who never had a coastline to soften them, treats a recited verse of unwritten Bedouin poetry as a more binding historical record than most government archives, insists that a shared plate of spiced rice and lamb is the correct unit of hospitality, and has spent the last century watching its own dialect ride a single family's conquest of the entire peninsula all the way into becoming the accent of the national government.

#### eras
---
Najdi Arabic changes with whoever's controlling the central Arabian plateau and how far their armies ride: a pre-Islamic Bedouin oral tradition that carried nabati poetry across the desert for 1,500 years before it was ever written down, a mid-18th-century religious-political alliance that founded the first Saudi state from a single oasis town, a British-Ottoman-Rashidi contest for the peninsula that briefly erased that state twice, a 20th-century reunification that carried Najdi speech to every corner of a newly declared kingdom, and a contemporary oil-funded era exporting Riyadh's dialect through national media, government, and a fast-modernizing Vision 2030 economy.
