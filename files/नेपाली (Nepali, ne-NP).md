Nepali is the language of a hill-kingdom army so effective that the very word for its soldiers became an international byword for elite infantry, and whose 18th-century unifying king took one look at a valley full of rival city-states and simply absorbed all of them into a single kingdom he ran from its grandest one. It grew out of a medieval Himalayan dialect promoted mainly because a warrior aristocracy happened to speak it, only produced its first firmly dated literary text in the late 14th century, and fought the British to a standstill closely enough that the resulting peace treaty is the reason Gurkha regiments still serve in the British and Indian armies today. It considers a lentil-rice-vegetable plate an everyday constitutional right, exports dumplings that half of South Asia now claims as their own, and endured a decade-long Maoist insurgency that ended not with foreign intervention but with the monarchy itself being voted out of existence.

#### eras
---
Nepali changes with whoever's controlling the mid-hills: an ancient Khasa Kingdom era that first promoted Khas Kura as a language of rule, a Malla era of rival Kathmandu Valley city-states, a Gorkha unification era launched by one king's conquest of the whole valley, an Anglo-Nepalese War era that fixed Nepal's modern borders, and a post-2008 Federal Democratic Republic era following the monarchy's abolition.
