D'ni is the language of a fictional subterranean civilization that turned out to have an unusually devoted real-world fanbase willing to keep speaking it decades after launch. Richard A. Watson joined Cyan, the game studio founded by brothers Rand and Robyn Miller in 1987 near Spokane, Washington, in September 1992 as Rand Miller's programming assistant, and went on to develop the D'ni language as part of his broader work on the Myst series, later serving as Riven's Game Designer and self-appointed "Continuity Guy." Cyan released Myst in 1993, introducing players to the D'ni, an ancient civilization living deep beneath the New Mexico desert whose written "Linking Books" could transport readers to other worlds; the language itself, an SVO tongue with vocabulary occasionally echoing Hebrew, was fleshed out across Myst's sequels, most notably Riven in 1997, where Rand Miller himself voiced the character Atrus alongside Sheila Goold as Catherine and John Keston as Gehn. The fan community proved remarkable in its staying power: Mysterium, an annual Myst and D'ni fan gathering, has convened every year since 2000, and when Cyan launched the MMO Myst Online: Uru Live on February 15, 2007, players could explore and help rebuild the D'ni civilization together inside the game's persistent virtual Cavern. After GameTap shut the service down in 2008, fan pressure led Cyan to reacquire the rights and relaunch it themselves in 2010, eventually releasing the game's source code to the community. Because D'ni has no gestural tradition of its own, and Cyan Worlds is based in Washington State, it borrows a thematic analog from that state's deaf-education history: the Washington School for the Deaf, founded near Vancouver on February 3, 1886.

#### eras
---
D'ni moves from a sparse in-game vocabulary to a fully developed constructed language with its own persistent fan-run virtual world: a Signed Language Analog Era representing Washington State's deaf-education history, a Written/Creation Era once Watson begins developing the language at Cyan, a Capital Era once Myst's release introduces the D'ni civilization to players, an Uru Live Launch Era once fans can explore and speak D'ni together online, and a Modern Community Relaunch Era once fan pressure leads Cyan to revive and open-source the game.
