Hakka is the language of a people who have spent two thousand years insisting they're just passing through, having fled the Yellow River basin in five distinct waves of migration only to keep landing on someone else's already-claimed farmland. It commemorates centuries of displacement by building circular earthen fortresses thick enough to withstand a siege and large enough to house eight hundred relatives under one roof, invented an entire cuisine around stuffing things that used to be wheat dumplings into tofu because wheat wouldn't grow in the hills it was pushed into, produced a wildly disproportionate share of modern China's revolutionaries, generals, and founding fathers for a group that supposedly never had a homeland of its own, and maintains, without apparent irony, that a name meaning "guest families" is a compliment.

#### eras
---
Hakka changes with wherever the next migration lands it and how much land its neighbors are willing to share: a Jin-dynasty exodus fleeing the Yellow River basin for the hills of Fujian and Jiangxi, a Tang-Song drift further south that filled out the mountain prefectures, a Ming-Qing wave that packed Guangdong's hill country and then went to war with its new neighbors over the leftovers, a nineteenth-century clan conflict that killed up to a million people over farmland, and now a state-sponsored preservation era broadcasting Hakka-language television and pop music to a diaspora mostly raised on Mandarin, Cantonese, or Malay.
