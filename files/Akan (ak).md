Akan is the language of a kingdom founded in 1680 under a tree, where a priest is said to have summoned a golden stool down from the sky to give a new king something to legitimately sit on, and that stool's authority proved durable enough that a British governor's decision to sit on it himself, two centuries later, triggered a war. That war, fought in 1900 and led by a queen mother in her sixties, killed roughly 3,000 people combined and ended with the kingdom's incorporation into a British colony, on the one condition that nobody outside the Akan world ever touch the stool again. The language's first complete Bible translation came out of a German missionary's decades of fieldwork in the 1870s, the same body of work that produced its first real grammar and dictionary. It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1957, the same year the country built around it declared independence from Britain. It ferments palm sap into wine, pounds cassava and plantain into a stretchy dough eaten by hand, and invented a two-row seed-sowing board game so well designed that variations of it are now played on every populated continent.

#### eras
---
Akan changes with whoever's controlling the forest-belt goldfields: an ancient Akan Kingdoms era of forest-state formation, a Kumasi/Ashanti Union era following Osei Tutu's unification, a British Gold Coast Colonial era after the fall of the Ashanti Empire, and a post-1957 Independent Ghana era.
