Chittagonian is the language of one of the world's oldest working ports, a natural harbor so ancient it turns up labeled on Ptolemy's map of the known world, later run for a stretch by a Buddhist king whose 10th-century capital there is attested mostly by one historian's say-so and otherwise lost to time. It has no standardized script of its own and gets written, when it's written at all, in borrowed Bengali letters, but it produced its first literary fingerprint anyway through a 17th-century local poet exiled into an Arakanese royal court, who left his translation unfinished at his death for a more famous successor to complete. It throws feasts built entirely around one boiled-down cow, wraps its port-town cosmopolitanism in centuries of Arabic, Persian, and Portuguese loanwords picked up from passing traders, and still gets waved off by some as "just a Bengali dialect" by people who have clearly never tried to follow a fast conversation in it.

#### eras
---
Chittagonian changes with whoever's controlling the port: an ancient trading-port era reaching back to a natural harbor known to Greek and Roman cartographers, a Buddhist Kingdom era of disputed local rule, an Arakanese/Mughal contested era of shifting sovereignty, a British colonial era including the 1930 armed uprising, and a post-1971 Bangladeshi era including the Chittagong Hill Tracts conflict.
