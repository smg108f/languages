Palestinian Arabic is the language of a strip of eastern Mediterranean coastline and highland that has been continuously fought over since before anyone thought to write any of it down, spoken by a people whose olive trees reportedly outlive every government that has ever claimed the land beneath them. It treats a shared plate of sumac-dusted chicken over flatbread as a homeland in miniature, keeps its embroidery patterns (tatreez) precise enough to identify a speaker's home village by thread count alone, and has kept its dialect alive across one of the world's largest and most dispersed refugee populations without ever losing track of which hill town it came from.

#### eras
---
Palestinian Arabic changes with whoever controls the hill country between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean: an ancient Canaanite-Jebusite settlement that predates written Arabic entirely, a 7th-century Islamic conquest that brought Arabic administration to Jerusalem, a Crusader interruption, four centuries of Ottoman rule, a British Mandate that redrew the region's borders on paper, a 1948 displacement that scattered the dialect across the Arab world's refugee camps, and a present-day era still defined by occupation, intifada, and war.
