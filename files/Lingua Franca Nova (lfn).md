Lingua Franca Nova, also called Elefen by its community, began as a teenager's private notebook project decades before anyone else saw it. C. George Boeree was born in Badhoevedorp, Netherlands, on January 15, 1952, immigrated to the United States with his family in 1956, and grew up on Long Island, New York; by 1965, at age thirteen, he had already started sketching an international auxiliary language built on a deliberately creolized Romance substrate, drawing vocabulary from French, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, and Catalan and grammar inspired by real-world creoles such as Papiamento, Haitian Creole, and Bislama, as well as the historical Mediterranean Lingua Franca pidgin. Boeree kept refining the project privately for over thirty years while building a career as a psychology professor at Shippensburg University in Pennsylvania, where he would go on to teach for three decades and publish a widely used free online textbook on personality theory. He finally published Lingua Franca Nova on the internet in 1998, and its small online community launched a dedicated wiki in 2005 to house its growing documentation. The language originally supported both Latin and Cyrillic orthographies as co-official scripts, a dual system that lasted until 2021, the same year Boeree died, on January 5, at his home in Shippensburg at age 68. Because Lingua Franca Nova has no gestural tradition of its own, and its creator spent his career in Pennsylvania, it borrows a thematic analog from that state's deaf history: the founding of the Pennsylvania School for the Deaf in Philadelphia in 1820.
