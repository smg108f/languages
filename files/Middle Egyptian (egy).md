Middle Egyptian is the language a usurping pharaoh's family gave Egypt after moving the capital to a new city near the Faiyum around 1991 BCE, a relocation made mostly so the king could keep a closer eye on Delta rebels and Nubian campaigns at once. Its finest surviving story follows a court official who overhears something he shouldn't at the old king's deathbed, panics, and flees into exile rather than risk a succession dispute, a tale ancient Egyptian readers kept copying and rereading for at least 750 years after it was composed around 1875 BCE. Its scribes, three centuries later, were blunt about people who didn't pay attention: one surviving reprimand accuses an idle student of behaving like someone "deaf and does not hear, to whom men make signs with the hand," which happens to be one of the oldest surviving written references to sign-based communication anywhere. Its speakers' most celebrated general once won a battle by taking the one mountain pass his own officers begged him not to use, catching an entire Canaanite coalition by surprise and forcing the Egyptian army's first fully documented tactical victory into military history. Its everyday spoken form kept evolving underneath it, but priests and scribes kept carving Middle Egyptian onto temple walls purely for ceremonial weight, the same way medieval European clerks kept writing legal Latin long after nobody used it to order dinner, right up until the last hieroglyphic inscription anyone ever carved, at a temple on the island of Philae, in 394 CE. It still gets taught in university seminars from a decoding breakthrough triggered by a black basalt slab found by French soldiers in 1799, and its most famous board game, played with grooved sticks and small pawns, is still sold in gift shops as a puzzle nobody's entirely sure how to win.

#### eras
---
Middle Egyptian changes with whoever's ruling the Nile: an Early Dynastic/Proto-hieroglyphic Era of names and labels only, an Old Kingdom/Old Egyptian Era once grammar first appears, a Middle Kingdom Classical Era once Middle Egyptian reaches its literary peak, a Late Egyptian Vernacular Era once everyday speech moves on while Middle Egyptian stays ceremonial, and a Ptolemaic-Roman Monumental Survival Era once Demotic takes over daily writing entirely.
