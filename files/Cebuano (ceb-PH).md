Cebuano is the language of an island kingdom legendarily founded by a half-Tamil prince who named his capital "Lion City" in Sanskrit, and whose most famous historical resident is a local chieftain who beat the first circumnavigation expedition in history to a standstill by killing its captain on a nearby beach. Its earliest surviving written record is, fittingly, a 160-word list scribbled down by that same expedition's own Italian chronicler days before things went badly for him, and it later ran an entire mid-20th-century film industry robust enough to rival the Manila-based one before quietly fading into a mostly-forgotten chapter of Philippine cinema. It roasts a whole pig with all its seasoning stuffed inside rather than rubbed on the skin, wraps its rice in woven palm-leaf pouches for portability, and floods its own streets every January with roughly two million people dancing a two-step shuffle named after the sound of a river current.

#### eras
---
Cebuano changes with whoever's controlling the Visayan seas: an ancient Rajahnate of Cebu era ruled from a legendary "Lion City" capital, an early contact era launched (and briefly interrupted) by Magellan's fatal 1521 visit, a Spanish colonial era after Legazpi's return, an American colonial and WWII era, and a post-1946 Philippine independence era including Cebuano cinema's mid-century golden age.
