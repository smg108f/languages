Vietnamese is the language of a nation that spent a millennium under Chinese administrative script, adapted those characters into a homegrown system nobody but scholars could read, and then discarded both in favor of a Portuguese-Jesuit-designed Latin alphabet stacked with diacritics like a circus balancing act, purely because it was easier to teach a peasant army to read in weeks rather than years. It treats a bowl of beef noodle soup as a complete national identity, was fought over in succession by imperial China, colonial France, and Cold War America, and emerged from all of it as one of Southeast Asia's most literate nations and the only one that writes its tones directly onto the vowels.

#### eras
---
Vietnamese changes with whoever's controlling the Red River Delta and points south: a millennium of Chinese administrative rule that imposed Classical Chinese as the language of officialdom, an independence era that produced a homegrown Chữ Nôm script, a French colonial period that replaced both Chinese-derived scripts with a Latin-based alphabet designed by missionaries, a war-and-partition era that split the country in two, and a post-1986 Đổi Mới reform era that reopened Vietnam to the world.
