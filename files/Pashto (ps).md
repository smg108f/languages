Pashto is the language of an empire founded in 1747 by a cavalry commander crowned near the very city, Kandahar, that would serve as his capital before his own dynasty moved the throne to Kabul a generation later. Its first surviving prose work, a syncretic 1570s treatise blending Pashto, Persian, Arabic, and Urdu, was written by a Sufi revolutionary who also invented thirteen new letters for the Pashto alphabet just so the manuscript would have somewhere to put its own sounds. It fought the same colonial power to a standstill across three separate wars, once losing a single 1880 battle so badly that a British regiment lost over 60 percent of its strength in an afternoon, and still turned the retreat into a folk legend. It gave the world a 1944 "lost treasury" of medieval poetry that scholars have spent eight decades arguing was actually written the same year it was "found." It drags a headless goat carcass around a flag on horseback and calls it a national sport, performs a war dance at weddings that started as literal battle preparation, and didn't get a deaf-community sign-language dictionary of its own until a foreign aid group compiled one in 1995.

#### eras
---
Pashto changes with whoever's controlling the Hindu Kush-Sulaiman corridor: an ancient, disputed pre-Islamic Pakhto era, a Ghaznavid Empire era centered on Ghazni, a Mughal-Safavid contest era fought largely over Kandahar itself, a Durrani Empire era launched by Ahmad Shah's coronation, and a post-1919 modern Afghan independence era.
