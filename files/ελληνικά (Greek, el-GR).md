Greek is the language whose oldest surviving writing turns up not on some poet's scroll but on a Bronze Age palace's inventory tablets, scratched down in a syllabic script that thousands of years of scholars couldn't read until one architect finally cracked it open in 1952. It got named the capital of its own modern nation-state in 1834 for a town that, at the time, had barely 7,000 residents living in what visitors politely called shacks, chosen mostly because a Bavarian archaeology enthusiast happened to be the new king's father. It fought a war of independence bloody enough to include a single massacre killing or enslaving up to 100,000 people on one island alone, then, more than a century later, tore itself apart again in a civil war that quietly outkilled the fighting that preceded it. It drowns everything in olive oil without apology, distills an anise spirit that turns cloudy the moment you add water, and produced both the marathon and the modern Olympics as unbothered gifts to the rest of the world.

#### eras
---
Greek changes with whoever's controlling the Aegean: a Mycenaean palace era first documented on Linear B tablets, a Classical/Hellenistic era of city-states and empire, a Byzantine era of Eastern Roman continuity, an Ottoman occupation era, and a post-1832 modern Greek state era including a war of independence and 20th-century civil war.
