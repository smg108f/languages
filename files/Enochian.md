Enochian is the "angelic language" that Elizabethan mathematician and court astrologer Dr. John Dee and his scryer Edward Kelley claimed to receive directly from angels during a string of visionary sessions conducted at Dee's home in Mortlake, on the south bank of the Thames, beginning in earnest in 1582. Kelley, gazing into a polished obsidian "shew stone," reported the first of twenty-four angelic contacts there, with the archangel Uriel among the first to appear, and the pair recorded the results meticulously in journals that Dee kept for years. The language's full alphabet, grammar, and vocabulary took shape over the following two years, with Dee and Kelley recording the complete Enochian script and its associated "calls" or "keys" by 1583, giving the world a fully-formed writing system that its own creators insisted they hadn't actually invented. Enochian went quiet for roughly three centuries after Dee's death in 1609 until London occultists William Westcott, William Woodman, and Samuel Mathers founded the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1888 and folded the entire angelic system into their own graded program of ceremonial magic, alongside Kabbalah, tarot, and astrology. Golden Dawn alumnus Aleister Crowley took Enochian furthest of anyone: in 1909, accompanied by Victor Neuburg in the Algerian desert, he ritually invoked all thirty of the system's "Aethyrs" one after another, assuming elaborate god-forms and reciting Enochian calls in a marathon of ceremonial gesture later published as The Vision and the Voice. The whole system finally got a proper academic once-over when linguist Donald Laycock published The Complete Enochian Dictionary in 1978, cataloguing four centuries of angelic vocabulary that had never once, by anyone's own account, been spoken by an actual human native speaker.
