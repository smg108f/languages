Italian is the language of a peninsula that didn't officially exist as a single country until a 19th-century poet's 14th-century breakup poem had already done the unification work for it, standardizing itself on the dialect of one city's greatest writer centuries before any government agreed on a flag. It runs an entire cuisine's worth of regional dialects mutually unintelligible enough that a Sicilian grandmother and a Venetian gondolier would each need subtitles for the other, spent decades fielding two rival capitals of taste and finance whose football clubs still settle the argument twice a season, and was, humiliatingly, the very last country in Europe to get around to legally recognizing its own sign language. It considers a well-made cup of coffee a matter of legally enforceable etiquette, treats a certain tomato-topped flatbread as a national gift to the entire planet, and somehow convinced Hollywood, decades running, that nothing conveys "romance" quite like its own accent.

#### eras
---
Italian changes with whoever's claiming to have unified the peninsula: a Late Antique era in which spoken Latin began drifting into regional vernaculars, a Trecento era in which Dante's Florentine verse gave the language a literary standard centuries before a country existed to speak it, a Risorgimento era of actual political unification, a Fascist era of aggressive linguistic and territorial nationalism, and a post-war Republican era that finally, if belatedly, extended full legal recognition to the Deaf community's own language.
