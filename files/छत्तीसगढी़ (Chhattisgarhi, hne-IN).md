Chhattisgarhi is the language of a mountain-ringed kingdom so naturally sheltered it sailed through centuries of Muslim invasions largely untouched, ruled from a capital its 12th-century founder built and named after himself, only to lose that capital's prominence to a rival town once the British showed up in 1845. It carried its literary tradition almost entirely by mouth for nearly a thousand years before anyone bothered writing a formal grammar of it, and it didn't get its own film industry until a Raipur-born filmmaker used the medium in 1965 to dramatize an inter-caste romance so controversial it needed the future Prime Minister's personal blessing to survive the backlash. It steams chickpea batter into little snack balls rather than frying them, keeps a percussion-driven harvest dance alive that doubles as courtship ritual, and has spent the last two and a half decades absorbing one of India's longest-running internal insurgencies almost entirely within its own borders.

#### eras
---
Chhattisgarhi changes with whoever's controlling the Mahanadi basin: an ancient pre-Kalachuri era of scattered petty states, a Kalachuri Kingdom era launched by Ratanpur's founding, a Mughal-vassalage era after the kingdom's 17th-century subjugation, a British colonial era that shifted regional prominence to Raipur, and a post-2000 Chhattisgarh statehood era following its split from Madhya Pradesh.
