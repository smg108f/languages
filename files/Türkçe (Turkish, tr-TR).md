Turkish is the language of a nation that discarded thirteen centuries of script tradition on one man's say-so and gave itself three months to learn the replacement, having previously spent six centuries running an empire that spanned three continents from a capital city that still straddles two of them. It treats a small cup of thick, unfiltered coffee as both a beverage and a legitimate fortune-telling device, considers a syrup-soaked layered pastry a form of hospitality diplomacy, and continues to argue, across a single bridge over the Bosphorus, about which side of the water its best football club calls home.

#### eras
---
Turkish changes with whoever's controlling Anatolia and its approaches: a Seljuk-era arrival that first brought Turkic speech into Anatolia, an Ottoman imperial era that layered heavy Arabic and Persian vocabulary onto court Turkish, a 20th-century Republican era that stripped much of that vocabulary back out again, and a 1928 alphabet reform that replaced the Arabic-derived Ottoman script with a Latin one virtually overnight.
