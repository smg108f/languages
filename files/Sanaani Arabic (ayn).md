Sanaani Arabic is the language of the highland capital that has been continuously inhabited for over 2,500 years and has apparently used most of that time to resist changing anything about itself, preserving classical grammatical features other Arabic dialects abandoned centuries ago while the rest of the world caught up to it by naming a coffee drink after its former seaport. It treats an afternoon spent chewing a mildly stimulating leaf in a mafraj sitting room as a full civic institution, considers a well-fortified walled Old City a birthright rather than a tourist attraction, and has spent the last decade proving that a language can keep its conservative charm even while the country speaking it endures one of the worst humanitarian crises on Earth.

#### eras
---
Sanaani Arabic changes with whoever's controlling the highland plateau around the capital: a pre-Islamic Sabaean and Himyarite kingdom era that left inscriptions in an entirely different script, an Islamic-era conquest that brought Arabic writing and a thousand-year run of Zaidi Imamate rule, an Ottoman colonial interval that turned a nearby fishing village into the coffee-trading capital of the world, a mid-20th-century republican revolution that finally ended the Imamate, a 1990 unification that restored Sana'a as capital of a single Yemeni state, and a present-day civil war that has made the dialect's homeland one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.
