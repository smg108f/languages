Kazakh is the language of a nomadic confederation founded in 1465 by two rebel sultans who simply rode away from their own khan with roughly 200,000 followers, and 134 years later staked down a permanent capital in a sacred desert city its rivals spent decades trying to take back. It endured a catastrophe in the 1720s so severe that its own people still call it simply "the Great Disaster," and two centuries later survived a Soviet collectivization famine that killed somewhere between 1.5 and 2.3 million of its speakers, nearly half the entire Kazakh population, inside four years. It didn't get a fully realized written literature until the 19th century, when a trio of scholars built one essentially from scratch using borrowed Russian type, and it has since switched its official alphabet four times, currently only halfway through a government-mandated switch to Latin script that isn't scheduled to finish until 2031. It ferments mare's milk into a sour, mildly alcoholic drink served at every gathering, wrestles a headless goat carcass off horseback as a national sport, and produced, in the mid-2010s, a single young singer whose reported seven-octave range made him one of the most-searched vocalists on the planet.

#### eras
---
Kazakh changes with whoever's controlling the steppe: an ancient Turkic Khaganate era of nomadic confederations, a Kazakh Khanate era founded by two breakaway sultans, a Russian Imperial Protectorate era following the Junior Zhuz's submission, a Kazakh SSR era under Soviet rule, and a post-1991 Independent Republic era.
