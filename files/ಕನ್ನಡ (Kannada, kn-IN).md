Kannada is the language of a Deccan plateau kingdom that built one of the largest cities on Earth at its medieval peak, only to have it reduced to ruins within a single afternoon by a coalition of rival sultanates, and has since produced more Jnanpith Award-winning authors than almost any other Indian language despite that setback. It renamed its own state after itself seventeen years after that state first existed under someone else's dynastic name, keeps a royal family's palace-and-heritage fortune technically undissolved long after the monarchy that built it, and races buffalo through ankle-deep mud as a matter of serious regional sporting tradition. It considers a many-dish rice preparation and a dense chickpea-flour fudge basic culinary identity markers, exported a small mining-town action franchise to the entire subcontinent's box office, and waited a genuinely absurd number of IPL seasons before its beloved home cricket team finally, actually won the thing.

#### eras
---
Kannada changes with whoever's controlling the Deccan plateau: a Kadamba founding era whose inscriptions are the language's earliest surviving records, a Rashtrakuta era that produced the first surviving Kannada literary treatise, a Hoysala era of temple-building patronage, a Vijayanagara imperial golden age, and a post-independence statehood era that first unified Kannada-speaking regions in 1956 and then renamed the whole state after the language in 1973.
