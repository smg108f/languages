British English is the aristocratic original from which an unruly colonial offshoot once declared independence, and it has never quite let that go. Having spent several centuries administering a quarter of the globe by committee, memorandum, and the occasional gunboat, it now expresses its residual imperial ambitions mainly through spelling corrections, tea etiquette, and a broadcasting corporation that insists its pronunciation is merely "received."
