Amharic is the language of a highland empire so committed to keeping its royal court on the move that it took until the 17th century for one emperor to build it a fixed capital, and whose earliest surviving literary compositions are victory songs written to celebrate a 14th-century king's military campaigns. It is one of the only major world languages whose speakers can point to an unbroken, uncolonized national history, having handed a European invading army one of the most decisive defeats of the entire colonial era in 1896, and it still writes itself in a syllabary descended from an ancient script that predates the language by over a thousand years. It keeps a spongy sourdough flatbread as the physical plate for nearly every meal, treats coffee preparation as a multi-hour ceremonial event rather than a beverage order, and produced the Rastafarian movement's entire theological premise simply by having an emperor whose pre-coronation name a Jamaican religious movement decided was prophetic.

#### eras
---
Amharic changes with whoever's controlling the Ethiopian highlands: a Solomonic Dynasty era launched by a 13th-century noble's claimed descent from Solomon, a Gondarine era once a permanent capital was finally built, a Modern Imperial era shaped by Ethiopia's decisive 1896 defeat of an invading Italian army, a Derg/Socialist era of military rule and civil war, and a post-1991 Federal Democratic era.
