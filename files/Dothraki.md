Dothraki exists because George R.R. Martin never bothered to build it himself. His novels gave the horse-riding Dothraki people only a few dozen invented words, so when HBO began adapting the books for television it turned to the Language Creation Society, which ran an open competition in 2009 inviting linguists to submit a fully realized grammar. David J. Peterson's roughly 30-page submission, complete with phonology, grammar, sample texts, and design rationale, beat out about 30 other entries, and he was hired to expand Martin's handful of words into a working language of some 3,000 words before the first episode aired. Game of Thrones premiered on HBO on April 17, 2011, putting spoken Dothraki in front of a mass television audience for the first time; the show would go on to become one of the network's biggest productions, with its 2019 series finale drawing a record 19.3 million same-night viewers. Peterson published a full course, Living Language: Dothraki, in 2014, giving fans outside the show a formal way to learn the language. Because Dothraki itself has no gestural tradition, it borrows a thematic analog from Malta, where the show's Season 1 Dothraki wedding sequence was filmed: the Maltese Parliament's March 16, 2016 recognition of Maltese Sign Language as an official language of Malta.
