Hindi is the language of a subcontinental population bloc so large that its native speakers alone would form the world's third most populous country, standardized only in the last two centuries out of a prestige register spoken in the villages just outside Delhi. It treats a wedding as a multi-day theatrical production, exports three-hour films with a built-in intermission as though runtime were a virtue rather than an inconvenience, considers a well-brewed cup of spiced milk tea a complete personality trait, and somehow convinced the rest of the world that a language descended from court Sanskrit and heavily seasoned with Persian is best known internationally through a film industry named after a different city's older nickname.

#### eras
---
Hindi changes with whoever's controlling the Indo-Gangetic plain around Delhi: a Sanskrit-descended Prakrit era predating any fixed "Hindi" identity, a Delhi Sultanate and Mughal era that folded Persian vocabulary and administration into a Khariboli-based koine, a colonial period that formally split that koine into Sanskritized Hindi and Persianized Urdu, a 1947 Partition that made the split a matter of national borders, a 1950 constitutional decision that made Hindi (in Devanagari) an official language of independent India, and a post-1991 liberalization era that sent Hindi cinema and, later, Hindi-language streaming content around the world.
