Sindhi is the language of a river-delta province so thoroughly conquered over the centuries that its own capital city has moved at least four times, most recently when an 18th-century ruler built a brand-new fort town on the ruins of an older one and called it Hyderabad, six decades before the British showed up and made it official one final time. It let a single 19th-century colonial committee settle, by vote, whether its script would be Arabic-derived or Devanagari-derived, watched more than a million of its own Hindu speakers leave for a neighboring country within a single year of Partition, and still argues, mostly amicably, over whether its very first feature film was shot in Bombay or Karachi. It keeps a spinach-and-lentil stew as its signature dish, produces one of Pakistan's most recognizable Sufi vocalists on a regular basis, and has spent nearly three centuries treating one 18th-century poet-saint's shrine as a pilgrimage site roughly equivalent to a national holiday.

#### eras
---
Sindhi changes with whoever's controlling the lower Indus: an Arab Conquest era that made Sindh the first Islamic polity in South Asia, a Mughal era once the region was folded into the empire as the Subah of Thatta, a Kalhora-Talpur era of competing local dynasties and a shifting capital, a British Colonial era opened by outright annexation, and a post-1947 Pakistan era defined by the mass departure of the language's Hindu speakers.
