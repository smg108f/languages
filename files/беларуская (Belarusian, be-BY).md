Belarusian is the language of a river-town principality first mentioned in the chronicles in 862, one whose own origin date is so uncertain that historians still just shrug and cite the earliest surviving reference. It spent centuries as the actual chancellery language of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, an administrative empire stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea, before quietly losing that status to Polish, and it only got its first printed Bible in 1517 from a Prague-based printer whose translation counted as one of the earliest vernacular Bibles anywhere in Europe. It lost an almost unthinkable one in three of its own people to Nazi occupation during the Second World War, absorbed roughly 60 percent of the fallout from a nuclear disaster that technically happened in a neighboring country, and still calls a fried-potato pancake its culinary flagship dish. It also, almost as a footnote, produced the video game about tanks that millions of people worldwide have played without ever learning where the company that made it was actually founded.

#### eras
---
Belarusian changes with whoever's controlling the upper Dnieper-Neman basin: an ancient Principality of Polotsk era first chronicled in 862, a Grand Duchy of Lithuania era during which Old Belarusian served as the chancellery language, a Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth era of gradual Polonization, a Russian Imperial/Soviet era, and a post-1991 independent Belarusian era.
