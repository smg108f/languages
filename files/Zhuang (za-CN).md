Zhuang is the language of a Tai-descended people whose most famous 11th-century leader spent the better part of a decade founding one short-lived kingdom after another, retaking a river town twice from imperial forces before an emperor's own hand-picked general finally hunted him down. It developed its own logographic script by borrowing and inventing Chinese-style characters starting around the Tang dynasty, calling the result "immature characters" in its own tongue, and kept producing folk songs, operas, and scripture in that script for a thousand years before China's first musical film turned one of its own legendary singers into a household name across an entire continent. It steams glutinous rice into five natural colors for its biggest spring festival, still tosses embroidered courtship balls between unmarried couples, and supplied, by some estimates, a quarter of the army behind one of the deadliest civil wars in human history.

#### eras
---
Zhuang changes with whoever's controlling the Guangxi highlands: an ancient Baiyue-era predating a distinct Zhuang identity, a Nanyue Kingdom era following Zhao Tuo's founding, a Song-dynasty vassalage era including Nong Zhigao's short-lived kingdoms, a Qing-era Taiping Rebellion era of major Zhuang participation, and a post-1958 Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region era.
