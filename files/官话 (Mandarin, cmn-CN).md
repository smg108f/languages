Mandarin is the language that decided meaning should depend on musical pitch, so that a single syllable said four different ways can mean "mother," "hemp," "horse," or "scold," apparently as a test of commitment. It has no alphabet to speak of, instead maintaining tens of thousands of individual characters that must each be memorized as its own small work of art, promotes itself nationwide through a decades-long standardized "common speech" campaign that has spent seventy years quietly correcting a billion people's hometown accents, and considers wordplay so sacred that entire Lunar New Year traditions are built around avoiding a number that merely sounds like the word for "death."

#### eras
---
Mandarin changes with whoever's ruling from Beijing and how officially "standard" the government insists everyone sound: a Mongol-founded capital that first put Beijing at the center of Chinese governance, a Qing court whose officials' speech became the aristocratic standard, a young republic assembling a national language by committee, a People's Republic that legislated a single "common speech" into hundreds of millions of mouths, and now a global media era exporting the language through livestreams and short-form video.
