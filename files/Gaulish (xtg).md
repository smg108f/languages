Gaulish is the Celtic language of ancient Gaul, attested almost entirely through inscriptions its own priesthood would have disapproved of: Julius Caesar records that the Druids considered it unlawful to commit their teachings to writing at all, preferring twenty years of memorized verse, even though they used Greek letters freely enough for ordinary business. Its earliest surviving texts appear anyway starting in the 3rd century BCE, Gallo-Greek inscriptions scattered up the Rhône valley around Marseille, well before anyone in Gaul was writing anything down for religious reasons. Its speakers found something like unity in 52 BCE, when the Aeduan oppidum of Bibracte hosted Vercingetorix's proclamation as war leader of the assembled Gallic tribes, a rebellion that ended catastrophically the same year at the Battle of Alesia, where an estimated 250,000 Gauls were killed or captured and Vercingetorix himself was marched off to a Roman prison cell to await execution. Its ritual life is preserved almost by accident in Pliny the Elder's Natural History, written around 77 CE, which describes Druids climbing an oak on the sixth day of the moon to cut mistletoe with a golden sickle, catching it in a white cloak before sacrificing two white bulls. Its decline under Roman rule was gradual rather than sudden: Jerome could still note around 387 CE that the Galatians of Anatolia spoke a language nearly identical to that of the Treveri back in Gaul, and Gregory of Tours, writing around 594 CE, recorded the destruction of a shrine in Auvergne still known locally by its Gallic name, Vasso Galatae, one of the last written traces of the language before it disappeared as a community tongue by the early 7th century. Its memory now survives mostly through Latin loanwords, place names, and a certain popular comic book series about a village of indomitable Gauls holding out against the Roman Empire.

#### eras
---
Gaulish changes with whoever's fighting Rome or getting absorbed by it: a Pre-Roman Tribal Era once Gallo-Greek inscriptions start appearing in the south, a Gallic Wars/Roman Conquest Era once Vercingetorix unites the tribes at Bibracte before losing at Alesia, a Roman Provincial Era once citizenship and Latin spread through the conquered territory, a Late Antique Bilingual Era once observers like Jerome note Gaulish still spoken alongside Latin, and a Terminal/Extinction Era once Gregory of Tours records one of its last written traces.
