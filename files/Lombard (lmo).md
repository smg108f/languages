Lombard is the language of a people named after invaders who never actually made its future biggest city their capital, choosing instead a fortified river town that held out against them for three straight years before finally falling in 572. Its oldest substantial literary work is a 1274 vision of the afterlife, written not in Latin but in the vernacular by a Milanese grammar teacher who apparently felt Hell, Purgatory, and Heaven all deserved a wider readership than the clergy alone could give them. Its dominant city was leveled by a Holy Roman Emperor in 1162, only for a coalition of Lombard cities to hand that same emperor a rout fourteen years later that a fortified war-cart parked at the center of their infantry line helped make possible. It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1805, and even then Milan's own school followed three others founded elsewhere in Italy first. Its dialect continuum still lacks a single agreed-upon spelling system to this day, which hasn't stopped its speakers from having their own celebrated 19th-century dialect poet, their own particular way of breading a veal cutlet, and their own sparkling wine grown specifically not to be confused with the French kind.

#### eras
---
Lombard changes with whoever's controlling the Po Valley: an ancient Insubres/Celtic Settlement era predating Roman conquest, a Kingdom of the Lombards era once Alboin's people take Pavia, a Lombard League/Communal era of allied city-states resisting imperial control, a Duchy of Milan era under the Visconti, and a Napoleonic/Austrian Lombardy era beginning with the Cisalpine Republic.
