Hausa is the language of a chain of West African city-states founded, according to their own origin story, by seven half-brothers whose father arrived from the east and killed the snake blocking the town's only well, and it has spent the following thousand-plus years running some of those exact same cities without interruption. It abandoned its own centuries-old Arabic-derived script for a colonial-era Latin one so thoroughly that the very word for "Western education" later got weaponized into the name of an entire insurgent group, keeps a horse parade running that some observers call the largest in the world, and settles at least one traditional wrestling match's worth of marriageability disputes the old-fashioned way. It considers a skewered spiced-meat snack a certified national export, produced Africa's single richest human being out of a Kano trading family, and turned public bare-knuckle boxing between fishermen and butchers into recognized professional sporting entertainment.

#### eras
---
Hausa changes with whoever's controlling the seven city-states and their neighbors: a Hausa Bakwai founding era launched by the legendary Bayajidda, a trans-Saharan trade era that built Kano into a major commercial hub, a Sokoto Caliphate era that unified the region's city-states under Fulani Islamic rule, a British colonial era that replaced the Arabic-derived Ajami script with the Latin-based boko script, and a post-independence era whose most defining recent chapter has been a devastating insurgency in the northeast.
