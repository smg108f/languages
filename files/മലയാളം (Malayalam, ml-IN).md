Malayalam is the language of a narrow coastal strip so committed to reading that it has held India's highest literacy rate for decades, elected the world's first-ever democratically-chosen communist government in 1957, and still runs on an economy substantially fueled by relatives working construction and hospitality jobs across the Gulf. It considers a Christian apostle's legendary 1st-century landing, an ancient Jewish community's arrival, and the region's own long-running Islamic trading links all part of the same unbothered coastal cosmopolitanism that later welcomed Portuguese cannons, Dutch ledgers, and British tea plantations in roughly that order. It races 100-rower wooden "snake boats" competitively, keeps alive one of the world's oldest documented martial arts, and exports a film industry so critically overachieving relative to its budget that international festival juries have stopped being surprised by it.

#### eras
---
Malayalam changes with whoever's controlling the Malabar Coast: an ancient Chera/Sangam era shared culturally with early Tamil, a Cheraman Perumal era that gave the language its own royal capital, a fragmented "Later Kingdoms" era of rival Kozhikode, Cochin, and Travancore courts, a colonial era launched by the first direct European sea route to India, and a post-independence statehood era whose first elected government made global political history.
