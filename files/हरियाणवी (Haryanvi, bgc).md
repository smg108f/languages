Haryanvi is the language of the land where the Mahabharata's own battlefield is supposedly located, later formalized into an actual post-Gupta capital by a father-son royal pair whose younger son went on to rule most of North India after his brother got assassinated. It spent most of its history as a spoken folk tradition of songs and swang theater rather than a written one, only got seriously documented once a 20th-century farmer-turned-poet started composing hundreds of folk dramas good enough to earn him the nickname "Kalidas of Haryana," and somehow hosted three separate empire-deciding battles at the exact same town over two and a half centuries. It turns wrestling into a village-by-village obsession serious enough to produce most of the country's recent Olympic medals in the sport, ladles ghee onto everything without embarrassment, and still gets represented on national television mostly through one dancer-turned-singer who became a reality-show household name.

#### eras
---
Haryanvi changes with whoever's controlling the Kurukshetra plain: an ancient Kuru Mahajanapada era tied to the legendary Mahabharata battlefield, a Vardhana dynasty era launched by a Thanesar-based capital, a Delhi Sultanate/Mughal era of shifting imperial control including the three Battles of Panipat, a British colonial era, and a post-1966 Haryana statehood era following its split from Punjab.
