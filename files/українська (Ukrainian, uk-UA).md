Ukrainian is the language of a nation whose imperial neighbor spent over a century periodically declaring, by official decree, that the language simply did not exist, banning it from print, theater, and classrooms while its speakers kept writing anyway. It survived a Soviet-engineered famine that killed millions of its own farmers within a single two-year stretch, and is now in the process of untangling its own sign language from the Soviet one it inherited, one de-Russified handshape at a time. It considers a beet-and-cabbage soup so definitively its own that it successfully lobbied UNESCO for emergency cultural-heritage protection the same year its capital came under direct military threat, has won the Eurovision Song Contest three separate times, and continues to hold its ground through the deadliest European land war since 1945.

#### eras
---
Ukrainian changes with whoever's controlling Kyiv and the steppe beyond it: a Kyivan Rus' founding era shared with its Slavic neighbors, a Polish-Lithuanian era that layered Western vocabulary onto the language, an Imperial Russian era of repeated legal suppression, a Soviet era of alternating Ukrainization and Russification campaigns, and a post-1991 independence era now defined by active resistance to a full-scale invasion.
