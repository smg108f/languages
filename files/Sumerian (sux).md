Sumerian is the language whose speakers invented writing itself, pressing wedge-shaped marks into wet clay at the city of Uruk sometime around 3200 BCE mostly to keep track of grain shipments and beer rations, only later realizing the same marks could record hymns, laws, and complaints about a bad harvest. Its greatest city spent a few centuries around 2900 BCE as the largest urban settlement on Earth, before the whole region eventually got absorbed into an empire built by a usurper king who claimed to have been found as a baby floating in a basket of reeds, a story the Book of Exodus would recycle centuries later for someone else. Its bureaucrats measured out labor, wages, and beer allotments using a base-60 counting system worked out on the twelve knuckles of one hand and the five fingers of the other, a hand-counting convention a later king named Shulgi formalized into official administrative practice around 2074 BCE. Its two rival city-states, Lagash and Umma, fought over a strip of farmland for roughly two centuries, a border dispute now remembered mainly for a stone monument showing vultures carrying off the losing side's severed heads. Its language stopped being anyone's mother tongue around 2000 BCE, replaced in daily speech by Akkadian, but scribes kept copying and reciting it in temples and scribal schools for another two thousand years, right up until the last dated cuneiform tablet, an astronomical almanac, was inscribed in 75 CE. It still gets studied by a small circle of specialists piecing together grammar from clay fragments, and its beer recipe, preserved in a hymn to the goddess who supposedly invented it, still gets brewed by modern enthusiasts curious what the world's oldest beer tasted like.

#### eras
---
Sumerian changes with whoever's ruling the alluvium: a Proto-Literate/Uruk Era once writing is first pressed into clay, an Early Dynastic City-States Era once rival cities like Uruk, Ur, and Lagash compete for dominance, an Akkadian Political Dominance Era once Sargon conquers the region, a Ur III/Sumerian Renaissance Era once a native dynasty briefly restores Sumerian rule, and a Liturgical/Scholarly Survival Era once Sumerian stops being anyone's mother tongue but keeps being copied by scribes.
