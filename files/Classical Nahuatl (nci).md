Classical Nahuatl is the language of the Aztec Empire, a Uto-Aztecan tongue that quietly reshaped English vocabulary with words like chocolate, tomato, coyote, avocado, chili, and mesquite long after the empire itself fell. Its capital, Tenochtitlan, was traditionally founded around 1325 CE on a marshy island in Lake Texcoco after the Mexica people saw an eagle perched on a cactus devouring a snake, exactly the sign their god Huitzilopochtli had promised them, and the city grew into the largest urban settlement in Mesoamerican history, home to as many as 200,000 people by 1519. Its writing system stayed pictographic and glyphic for most of its history rather than fully alphabetic, and one of its clearest surviving examples is the Templo Mayor Dedication Stone, carved in 1487 CE to mark the completion of the Aztec's Great Temple, showing rulers Tizoc and Ahuitzotl piercing their own earlobes in ritual bloodletting before the earth goddess Tlaltecuhtli. That same bloodletting gesture anchors Nahuatl's most spectacular ritual: the New Fire Ceremony, performed once every 52 years to stave off the end of the world, held for the final time in pre-Hispanic history on November 1, 1507, when priests climbed the Hill of the Star under Moctezuma II and rekindled every fire in the empire from a single new flame. The empire that built all this collapsed with startling speed: Spanish conquistadors and their Indigenous allies laid siege to Tenochtitlan for 93 days before the city surrendered on August 13, 1521. Classical Nahuatl outlived the empire for a while as a written and administrative language, most extensively documented in the Florentine Codex, a massive bilingual Nahuatl-Spanish encyclopedia of Aztec life compiled by Franciscan friar Bernardino de Sahagún and Nahua scholars, completed around 1577, before gradually giving way to the many modern Nahuan varieties still spoken by roughly 1.7 million people in Mexico today.

#### eras
---
Classical Nahuatl shifts with whoever's running Tenochtitlan or documenting it afterward: a Tenochtitlan Founding Era once the capital is established, a Glyphic/Written Era once monuments like the Dedication Stone are carved, a Ritual/Renewal Era once the New Fire Ceremony's final performance is documented, a Spanish Conquest/Collapse Era once Tenochtitlan falls, and a Colonial Documentation Era once the Florentine Codex is completed.
