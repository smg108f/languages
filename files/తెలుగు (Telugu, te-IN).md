Telugu is the language of a Deccan plateau civilization so devoted to poetic craftsmanship that it took three separate poets across four centuries just to finish translating one epic, splitting itself in 2014 into two states rather than compromise on the matter of who gets to claim a shared capital city. It calls itself "the Italian of the East" for its vowel-heavy musicality, considers a fiery sun-dried mango-and-lentil relish a birthright, exported a single film franchise about a mythical warrior-prince that outgrossed most of Hollywood's output for that year, and somehow made a 40-word Oscar-winning dance number the most globally recognized thing to ever come out of the Godavari delta.

#### eras
---
Telugu changes with whoever's controlling the eastern Deccan: an ancient Brahmi-descended era predating any fixed "Telugu" identity, a Kakatiya-dynasty golden age that made Warangal a political and architectural capital, a Vijayanagara and Golconda Sultanate interval that layered Persian and Deccani vocabulary onto court Telugu, a colonial and Nizam-ruled era that split administrative loyalties, and a post-independence period that, after decades of agitation, split the Telugu-speaking region itself into two states in 2014.
