Finnish is the language of a country whose oldest city was founded in 1229 specifically because a river mouth offered a defensible harbor, and which then served as the nation's capital for nearly six centuries before a Russian emperor decided it sat too close to Sweden for comfort and moved the seat of government elsewhere. It had no writing system of its own until a Lutheran clergyman, trying to get the Reformation's ideas across to people who couldn't read Latin, printed a spelling primer in 1543 and followed it five years later with the entire New Testament. Its national epic wasn't an ancient text at all but a 19th-century scholar's compilation of oral folk poetry, stitched together specifically to give a country under Russian rule something to feel proud of. Its most famous war was a 105-day stand against an invading army roughly three times its size, fought by ski troops in white camouflage in temperatures that dropped to nearly minus 45 degrees, and it lost anyway, though not before inflicting far heavier losses than anyone expected. It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1846, founded by a Deaf man himself. It drinks more coffee per capita than almost anywhere on Earth, eats a candy made of salty ammonium chloride that visitors reliably hate on first try, and treats sitting naked in a wood-heated room hot enough to make your ears ring as a completely unremarkable Tuesday evening activity.

#### eras
---
Finnish changes with whoever's controlling the Baltic's northern shore: an ancient Finnic-settlement era predating recorded history, a Turku/Medieval Founding era once Swedish crusaders establish control, a Swedish Crown Rule era, a Grand Duchy of Finland era after Sweden cedes the territory to Russia, and a post-1917 Independent Republic era.
