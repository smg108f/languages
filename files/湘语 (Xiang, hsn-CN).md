Xiang is the language of a province that decided spice was a personality trait, treating chili peppers as a dietary cornerstone and citing 3.3 million tons of annual consumption as though it were a badge of honor. It produced an outsized share of modern China's revolutionaries and generals, a fact locals attribute directly to the food, keeps two mutually distinct sub-dialects that split centuries ago over whether to keep pronouncing certain consonants the old way, and has spent the last two decades exporting reality-television formats and talent-show tears to the rest of the country from a single broadcaster in Changsha.

#### eras
---
Xiang changes with whoever's ruling the Yangtze's southern reaches and how loudly Hunan asserts its own fighting spirit: an ancient Chu kingdom whose songs and shamanistic poetry still echo through the language, a thousand-year-old academy that made Changsha a center of classical learning, a homegrown army that saved a dynasty from a peasant rebellion, a province that endured some of the war's bloodiest defensive battles, and now a media powerhouse broadcasting talent shows and variety television across the Chinese-speaking world.
