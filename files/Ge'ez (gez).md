Ge'ez is the Ethiopian and Eritrean liturgical language whose fortunes have tracked one highland trading kingdom since the 1st century CE, when Aksum became its capital and turned an otherwise unremarkable stretch of the Horn of Africa into a power Mani of Persia ranked alongside Rome, Persia, and China. Its script picked up full vowel notation sometime around King Ezana's reign in the 4th century, and the proof survives on the Ezana Stone itself, which documents Ezana's conversion to Christianity in the same breath as it happens to be the oldest fully vocalized Ge'ez inscription anyone has found. Its liturgical performance got a whole physical vocabulary in the 6th century once Saint Yared, according to church tradition, received a vision in paradise and came back with the melodies, hand-staff gestures, sistrum shakes, and drumbeats that make up Aqwaqwam, the ritual chant-and-dance system Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox clergy still perform today essentially unchanged. Its status as anyone's native tongue quietly expired sometime before 1270, when Yekuno Amlak overthrew the Zagwe dynasty and founded the Solomonic line from an Amhara-region power base, letting Amharic finish displacing Ge'ez in the marketplace while the church kept it for scripture. Its literary output kept going anyway: the Kebra Nagast, tracing the Solomonic dynasty back to Solomon and the Queen of Sheba, got its now-standard form around 1321 from a cleric named Yeshaq of Aksum, and over a century later Emperor Zar'a Ya'qob had the Fetha Nagast law code translated into Ge'ez from an Arabic original. Its manuscripts also preserve books that vanished everywhere else, most famously the Book of Enoch, which survives complete only in Ge'ez translation despite being lost in its original language for over a thousand years. Its congregations, from Addis Ababa to Asmara to the diaspora churches of Washington, D.C. and Stockholm, still hear the Divine Liturgy chanted in it every Sunday, understood by the clergy and largely mysterious to everyone else.

#### eras
---
Ge'ez changes with whoever's ruling from Aksum or reshaping its liturgy: an Aksumite Founding Era once Aksum becomes the kingdom's capital, a Christian Aksumite Era once King Ezana converts and vocalizes the script, a Liturgical Chant Era once Saint Yared originates the Aqwaqwam chant-and-dance tradition, a Solomonic/Liturgical-Only Era once Yekuno Amlak's dynasty lets Amharic take over as the spoken vernacular, and a Modern Diaspora Era once congregations spread the liturgy worldwide.
