Wu is the language of a river delta that decided sophistication was a birthright, speaking in a softer, more slurred register that locals insist proves Jiangnan culture was always the tasteful one. It merges syllables together through a tone-sandhi system so aggressive that entire sentences can shed most of their individual tones the moment they're strung together, quietly maintains that Shanghai was cosmopolitan before cosmopolitan was a word anyone else used, and has spent the last several decades watching its own schoolchildren taught almost exclusively in Mandarin, a phenomenon it discusses at length in a dialect fewer of them can still speak.

#### eras
---
Wu changes with whoever's controlling the Yangtze Delta and how open its port cities are to the world: rival ancient kingdoms fighting over the same fertile river delta, an independent Wuyue kingdom that built the canals still shaping the region, a Southern Song court that fled south and turned Jiangnan into China's cultural and economic heart, a treaty port carved out of a fishing village, and now a global financial hub broadcasting Shanghai glamour to the rest of the world.
