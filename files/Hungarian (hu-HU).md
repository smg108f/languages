Hungarian is the language of seven nomadic tribes who rode out of the Ural region, settled a river basin surrounded entirely by unrelated language families, and got their first king crowned in a river town in the year 1000 by a pope who'd apparently decided the whole arrangement was worth blessing. It once carved its words into wood and bone using a runic alphabet borrowed from Central Asian steppe scripts, only produced its oldest surviving continuous text as a funeral sermon written by a single anonymous scribe around 1192, and lost a battle so catastrophically in 1526 that "more was lost at Mohács" became the country's standing idiom for total ruin. It floods more Olympic medals into water polo than arguably any other nation its size, distills a fruit brandy strong enough to double as a verb, and still runs a national deaf-education tradition traceable to one 19th-century jurist's founding of a residential school in a small town on the Danube.

#### eras
---
Hungarian changes with whoever's controlling the Carpathian Basin: an ancient Magyar tribal era of steppe migration, a Kingdom of Hungary era launched by Stephen I's coronation, an Ottoman-Habsburg era opened by catastrophic defeat at Mohács, a Habsburg/Austro-Hungarian era of dual-monarchy administration, and a post-1946 republican era including the 1956 Revolution.
