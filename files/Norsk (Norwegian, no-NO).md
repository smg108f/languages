Norwegian is the language of a kingdom that didn't exist until a 9th-century warlord won a single naval battle decisive enough that the sagas remember it by name, and whose first real capital was a trading post a Viking king founded specifically because a river bend gave it natural defenses against attack. Its oldest substantial manuscript is a collection of sermons copied around 1200, written not to preserve the language for its own sake but because someone in a Bergen monastery needed something to preach from. Four centuries of Danish rule left it writing in what was functionally Danish, a colonial hangover so persistent that the country ended up with two official written standards: one a linguist built from scratch out of rural dialects specifically to replace the Danish-derived version, and neither has managed to fully dislodge the other since. It lost roughly 10,000 people during five years of German occupation in WWII, a wound still marked every year on the anniversary of its 1945 liberation. It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1825, founded by a Deaf Trondheim native who'd had to travel to Copenhagen just to get an education himself. It cures salmon instead of cooking it, distills a spiced spirit specifically to go with fatty holiday food, and considers cross-country skiing less a sport than a basic form of winter transportation.

#### eras
---
Norwegian changes with whoever's controlling the fjords: an ancient unification era beginning with Harald Fairhair's victory at Hafrsfjord, a Nidaros/Medieval Kingdom era once Trondheim becomes the royal seat, a Kalmar Union/Danish Rule era lasting four centuries, a Union with Sweden era following the Napoleonic Wars, and a post-1905 Independent Kingdom era.
