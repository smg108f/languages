Somali is the language of a 10th-century trading sultanate whose founder minted his own currency and whose descendants ruled a Swahili Coast city-state that dominated the regional gold trade for centuries, all while the language itself spent the next thousand years functioning almost entirely without a settled writing system. When a Somali sultan finally invented a phonetically tailored alphabet for it in the early 1920s, specifically to avoid using either the colonizers' Latin script or the clergy's Arabic one, a later military government scrapped it fifty years later in favor of Latin anyway, launching a national literacy campaign to make the switch stick. It fought a war with its neighbor over a single disputed region, then turned around and visited a genocide on its own northern clan that killed as many as 200,000 of its own citizens, and still didn't get a deaf-community school of its own until a foreign aid worker built one three years after the state itself had collapsed. It ferments a spongy pancake-bread overnight before anyone eats it, still tells its oldest stories through a sung women's poetry form passed mother to daughter, and produced both an Academy Award-nominated Hollywood actor and the continent's largest money-transfer network out of the same small, long-stateless nation.

#### eras
---
Somali changes with whoever's controlling the Horn of Africa coast: an ancient Sultanate of Mogadishu era launched by a Swahili Coast trading city-state, a Sultanates era of Ajuran and Adal rule across the interior, a colonial partition era dividing the territory between Italy, Britain, and France, a post-1960 independent Somali Republic era, and a post-1991 civil war/fragmentation era.

#### signed

global-signers :: no comprehensive national count published

signer-locations :: Somalia and Somaliland (Mogadishu, Hargeisa, Borama)

governing-bodies ::
traces to the Annalena School for the Deaf, established 1997 in Borama by graduates of an earlier Kenyan-Somali deaf school in Wajir; the Somali Deaf Union (SODO, est. 2006) and Somali National Association for the Deaf (SONAD, est. 2007) advocate nationally; historically influenced by Kenyan Sign Language
