Biblical Hebrew is the Northwest Semitic language of the Hebrew Bible, a tongue that stopped being anyone's mother language many centuries ago yet somehow never stopped being read out loud. Its political anchor is Jerusalem, which King David is traditionally said to have captured and made his capital around 1000 BCE, a decision that turned a modest hill town into the enduring religious center of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam alike. Its written record shows up soon after in the Gezer Calendar, a small limestone tablet listing the agricultural seasons and inscribed around 925 BCE, one of the earliest texts confidently identified as Hebrew rather than a closely related regional script. Its oldest surviving biblical text, meanwhile, is a piece of jewelry: the Ketef Hinnom silver scrolls, tiny amulets inscribed around 600 BCE with the Priestly Blessing from the Book of Numbers, which is still recited today by kohanim who raise their hands above the congregation in the ritual gesture known as Birkat Kohanim, spreading their fingers into the deliberate "windows" through which the blessing is said to flow. The kingdom whose capital and language these traditions describe did not last: Babylon destroyed the First Temple and exiled much of Judah's population in 586 BCE, a catastrophe so total that biblical Hebrew itself began drifting from daily speech into the more purely liturgical and literary role it has occupied ever since. Its texts kept surfacing long after anyone still spoke the language natively, most dramatically in 1947, when a Bedouin shepherd stumbled into a cave at Qumran and found the Dead Sea Scrolls, suddenly handing scholars a trove of Biblical Hebrew manuscripts a thousand years older than anything they'd had before.

#### eras
---
Biblical Hebrew shifts with whoever's running Jerusalem or unearthing it centuries later: a Davidic Capital Era once Jerusalem becomes the kingdom's capital, an Early Hebrew Written Era once inscriptions like the Gezer Calendar appear, a Priestly Blessing/Ritual Era once the Ketef Hinnom scrolls attest the Birkat Kohanim text, a First Temple Destruction/Babylonian Exile Era once Jerusalem falls, and a Modern Rediscovery Era once the Dead Sea Scrolls surface at Qumran.
