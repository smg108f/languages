Polish is the language whose oldest surviving sentence was written down entirely by accident, when a 13th-century monk transcribing a Latin monastery register paused to note, almost as an aside, that a local peasant had told his wife to let him grind the millstone while she rested, adding "this is in Polish" so nobody would mistake it for bad Latin. Its first king was crowned in a city that stayed the nation's capital for over five and a half centuries before a 16th-century monarch, tired of ruling a country that had suddenly doubled in size, packed up the court and moved two hundred kilometers north. Its 20th century included a simultaneous invasion from two directions in 1939, a forest massacre of thousands of its army officers by one of those invaders, and a 63-day uprising in its capital that killed up to 180,000 civilians and left the city so thoroughly leveled that photographs of it are still used to illustrate the word "rubble." It didn't get an organized school for its Deaf community until 1817, founded by a priest who'd learned to sign in France. It stuffs dumplings with potato and cheese, distills its national spirit from rye or potatoes and sometimes flavors it with a single blade of bison grass, and treats a bowl of fermented rye soup as a legitimate breakfast.

#### eras
---
Polish changes with whoever's controlling the Vistula basin: a Kingdom of Poland era beginning with the first crowned king, a Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth era after the Union of Lublin, a Partitioned Poland era once Prussia, Russia, and Austria carved the country up, a WWII/Nazi-Soviet Occupation era, and a post-1918/1945 Independent Poland era.
