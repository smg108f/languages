Zulu is the language of a kingdom stitched together in the early 19th century by a warlord who absorbed neighboring clans into a single nation of tens of thousands through a mix of conquest and diplomacy, then founded a permanent royal capital that the British burned to the ground within six years of its establishment. Its earliest substantial written form came not from its own royal scribes but from an American missionary, who had the Gospel of Matthew translated into it in 1848, decades before the whole Bible followed in 1883. It won one of the most lopsided battles against a colonial army in the whole of 19th-century Africa, then lost the war anyway a few months later when the same army captured and razed its capital. It ferments its maize and sorghum into a beer once called, by its own last independent king, simply "the food of the Zulus," settles cattle-based board-game disputes with pieces literally called cows, and gave the world an a cappella singing style so influential that a foreign pop star built an entire chart-topping album around it in 1986.

#### eras
---
Zulu changes with whoever's controlling the KwaZulu-Natal coast: an ancient Nguni settlement era of early Bantu farming communities, a Zulu Kingdom era launched by Shaka's unification of the Nguni clans, a British Zululand colonial era beginning with the Anglo-Zulu War, a Union of South Africa era incorporating Natal and Zululand, and a post-1994 democratic Republic era.
