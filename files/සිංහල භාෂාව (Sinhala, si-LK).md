Sinhala is the language of an island kingdom whose founding legend involves a banished prince sailing in with 700 followers and somehow still getting credit for starting the whole national story, later formalized into an actual capital by a grandson who built it up from a minister's riverside campsite. It got its script from a monk who happened to be the emperor of India's own son, spent the next thousand-plus years turning Brahmi letters into their current rounded form, and eventually watched its last independent hill-kingdom get signed away to the British in a treaty whose fine print triggered a rebellion two years later. It piles rice under a half-dozen curries at every meal without complaint, turns leftover flatbread into a stir-fried street food loud enough to be sold by the sound of the chopping alone, and spent twenty-six years in a civil war whose final death toll still depends entirely on which report you're reading.

#### eras
---
Sinhala changes with whoever's controlling the island: a legendary founding era beginning with Prince Vijaya's arrival, an Anuradhapura Kingdom era launched by a formal capital city, a Polonnaruwa/shifting-capital era after Anuradhapura's fall, a Kandyan Kingdom era of the last independent Sinhalese monarchy, and a British/post-1948 independence era including the Sri Lankan Civil War.
