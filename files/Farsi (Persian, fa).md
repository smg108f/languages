Persian is the language of an empire so confident in its own permanence that it ran four capital cities at once, only to spend the next fourteen centuries writing itself down in three completely different scripts as first Elamite-trained scribes, then Zoroastrian priests, then Arab-influenced calligraphers each decided the previous alphabet wasn't quite doing the job. It survived an Arab conquest that could easily have erased it outright, credits one poet's single 50,000-couplet epic with personally rescuing the language from bureaucratic Arabic hegemony, and later exported a Sufi mystic's verse so effectively that centuries on it still shows up truncated and mistranslated across the internet's inspirational-quote economy. It treats a crisped-up layer of rice as the single most contested subject in any household kitchen, adapted someone else's board game into a small simulated war it then exported back out to the entire planet, and remains, under decades of sanctions and considerable domestic financial opacity, a remarkably difficult language in which to determine who actually has the money.

#### eras
---
Persian changes with whichever empire currently claims the Iranian plateau: an Achaemenid founding era whose kings ran four capitals simultaneously, a Sassanid era that wrote the language in a Zoroastrian priestly script, an early Islamic era that replaced that script wholesale after the Arab conquest, a Safavid era that made Twelver Shi'ism a matter of state and linguistic identity, a Pahlavi era of forced Westernizing modernization, and a post-1979 Islamic Republic era that reversed a fair amount of it again.
