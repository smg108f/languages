Swedish is the language of a nation whose oldest surviving literature was chiseled into a single boulder around 800 CE, an inscription so long and so cryptically composed that scholars are still arguing over what it actually says. It got its enduring capital in 1252 from a regent who built a fortified settlement on a set of islands, a choice so sound that the country never seriously considered moving the seat of government again. It once ran an empire so aggressive that its king marched an army deep into Russia, only to lose virtually the whole force in a single afternoon in 1709, an afternoon that ended Sweden's run as a European great power for good. It hasn't fought a war since 1814, a stretch of peace so long that most Swedes alive today have never lived through one, and it spent that peace inventing a flat-pack furniture empire, one of history's best-selling children's book characters, and a coffee break so culturally load-bearing it has its own name. It ferments herring until the cans bulge, distills grain into a spirit named for water, and once got so nervous about a beer-heavy Christmas that a chemist invented an entire alternative soft drink just to cut into the holiday's alcohol sales.

#### eras
---
Swedish changes with whoever's controlling the Baltic: an ancient Viking Age era first documented on carved runestones, a unified Kingdom of Sweden era following Olof Skötkonung's rule, an early modern Vasa-dynasty era, a Swedish Empire/Age of Greatness era of 17th-century military expansion, and a post-1905 modern constitutional era following the dissolution of the union with Norway.
