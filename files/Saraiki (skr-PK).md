Saraiki is the language of a walled desert city so old and so frequently besieged that its own sun-temple economy reportedly generated nearly a third of an entire medieval state's tax revenue before religious rivals tore it down, earning the place a reputation as the "Frontier House of Gold" centuries before anyone called it the "City of Saints." It kept a distinct trader's script alive for commercial ledgers well into the 20th century before British administrators quietly swapped it out for a Perso-Arabic alternative, only picked its own modern name in the 1960s at a founding meeting of regional scholars, and produced, in one 19th-century Sufi mystic's desert-retreat poetry, a body of verse still treated as the language's own Shakespeare. It keeps a saffron-and-milk fudge sweet as its most famous export, still turns entire wedding parties over to a shoulder-swaying folk dance native to the region, and quietly runs on some of Pakistan's largest surviving feudal landholdings.

#### eras
---
Saraiki changes with whoever's controlling the lower Indus-Chenab confluence: an ancient Multan era centered on its famed Sun Temple, an Islamic Emirate era after Multan became a regional capital, a Delhi Sultanate/Mughal era of shifting imperial control, a Bahawalpur princely-state era of Abbasi Nawab rule, and a modern Saraiki identity era launched by the 1960s naming movement.
