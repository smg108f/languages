Javanese is the language of an island civilization that built two ceremonially competing royal courts a few kilometers apart out of the same collapsed sultanate rather than pick one, still runs a strict multi-tier grammar of politeness where the words for basic concepts like "eat" or "go" change depending on whose social rank you're addressing, and spent roughly a millennium writing itself in a curling Brahmi-descended alphabet before quietly swapping it out for the Latin one nobody had to ban. It stages epic battles between painted leather puppets accompanied by an orchestra of tuned bronze gongs as a matter of routine village entertainment, treats a wax-resist cloth pattern as UNESCO-certified heritage rather than mere fashion, and lost an estimated one in thirty of its own people to disease and famine during a five-year rebellion against Dutch rule led by a prince now honored on banknotes and street signs across the country he never got to rule.

#### eras
---
Javanese changes with whichever court controls the island's interior: an Old Javanese era launched by the adoption of the Kawi script, a Singhasari-Kediri interval of shifting inland kingdoms, a Majapahit imperial golden age that briefly unified much of the archipelago, a colonial-era Mataram Partition that split one sultanate into rival Yogyakarta and Surakarta courts, and a post-independence Modern Javanese era absorbed into a national Indonesian identity.

#### signed
---
signed-name :: Indonesian Sign Language (Bahasa Isyarat Indonesia, BISINDO) — Java varieties

global-signers :: part of Indonesia's estimated 2.5M deaf population

signer-locations :: Java (pan-island); distinct Yogyakarta and Jakarta urban varieties, roughly 65% lexically cognate but grammatically diverging

governing-bodies ::
not Indonesia's officially government-backed system (schools instead teach SIBI, a signed-Indonesian code); BISINDO's Java varieties trace to community deaf networks documented from the 1950s or earlier
