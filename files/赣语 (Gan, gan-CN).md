Gan is the language of a province that has spent fourteen centuries being the place other people pass through on their way to becoming famous elsewhere, watching two great waves of Yellow River refugees settle in, absorb, and eventually forget they were ever migrants at all. It fired the kilns that supplied porcelain to emperors and European aristocrats alike from a single riverside town, launched the armed uprising the state now dates its entire military to from a single night in its provincial capital, considers freshwater fish from its own lake system a more honest test of a cook than anything caught in the ocean, and prefers its chili peppers served with the blunt, unadorned heat of a place that has never felt the need to dress spiciness up as anything other than spiciness.

#### eras
---
Gan changes with whichever wave of northern refugees has most recently settled the Gan River valley and how loudly the porcelain kilns are firing: a Jin-dynasty exodus fleeing invasion that planted Old Chinese in the region for the first time, a Tang-Song wave of Middle Chinese migrants that made Nanchang's speech the province's prestige standard, a Ming dynasty that turned a single riverside town into the emperor's porcelain supplier, a single night in the provincial capital that the state now dates its entire armed forces to, and a contemporary economy still exporting copper, lithium, and smartphone components from the same river basin that once exported dynastic tableware.
