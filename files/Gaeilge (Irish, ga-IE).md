Irish is the language whose earliest surviving writing wasn't written to be read at all, carved as vertical notches into the edges of standing stones sometime around the late 4th century, a code so specifically suited to stone that historians still argue over whether anyone ever actually wrote it on parchment first. Its symbolic seat of power was a hilltop where, according to tradition, 142 kings once reigned before a single 980 battle there let a Gaelic high king finally break the Viking-held Kingdom of Dublin's grip on the country. Its 19th century was defined by a famine that killed roughly a million people and drove another million and a quarter to emigrate, a demographic wound so severe the island's population still hasn't fully recovered nearly two centuries later. Its 20th century opened with a six-day uprising in 1916 that killed 485 people, most of them civilians, and closed a war for independence five years later that killed over 2,300 more. It didn't get a school for its Deaf community until 1816, founded after a doctor stumbled across twenty-one deaf children in a Dublin workhouse. It drinks a stout so associated with the country that tourists mispronounce the brewery's name on purpose, eats potatoes reconstituted into cake after nearly losing the crop entirely, and considers hurling, a sport played with a wooden stick and a small leather ball at speeds that regularly draw blood, a matter of parish pride rather than recreation.

#### eras
---
Irish changes with whoever's controlling the island: an early Ogham/Primitive Irish era predating recorded history, a Gaelic High-Kingship/Tara era once the Uí Néill dynasties assert dominance, an Anglo-Norman Invasion era after 1169, an Act of Union/United Kingdom era once Ireland is absorbed into Great Britain, and a post-1921 Irish Free State/Independence era.
