Tamil is the language of a civilization so committed to its own literary antiquity that it treats its oldest surviving poetry as older than most of the empires that later tried to rule over it, and was the first language India's government ever bothered to legally certify as "classical." It considers a fermented rice-and-lentil batter steamed or griddled into a dozen forms a complete cuisine unto itself, keeps a 3,000-year-old bull-grabbing harvest sport alive through bans, courts, and protests alike, exported a film industry whose biggest star built a decades-long career partly on the strength of flipping his sunglasses on-screen, and remains fiercely claimed on both shores of a strait separating two countries that fought an entire civil war partly in its name.

#### eras
---
Tamil changes with whoever's controlling the far south of the subcontinent: a Sangam era whose surviving anthologies are treated as literature's own founding scripture, a Chola imperial age that spread Tamil temple architecture and administration overseas, a Vijayanagara-Nayak interval, a colonial period, and a post-independence Dravidian-movement era that eventually renamed an entire state to make the language's primacy explicit.
