Old Church Slavonic is the language two Byzantine brothers from Thessalonica built almost overnight in 863 CE, inventing an entirely new alphabet on the emperor's orders so they could translate scripture for a Central European prince who'd asked Constantinople for missionaries his own people could actually understand. Its speakers got kicked out of that prince's kingdom within a generation, Great Moravia's German-aligned clergy having little patience for a liturgy in Slavic rather than Latin, so Cyril and Methodius's exiled students regrouped in Bulgaria instead, where Tsar Simeon I turned his new capital into the language's real intellectual home starting in 893 CE. Its later students, evidently unbothered by the sheer effort already sunk into one custom alphabet, invented a second one shortly afterward loosely based on Greek capital letters, which is the one that stuck and now bears Cyril's name despite crediting the wrong brother. Its texts reached Kievan Rus' in 988 CE when Grand Prince Vladimir baptized his realm wholesale, and the earliest fully dated manuscript to survive that conversion, a lavishly illuminated Gospel book finished in 1057 for a Novgorod official, is still the oldest precisely dated East Slavic book anyone has. Its monasteries governed silence and gesture strictly enough that Kiev's own Monastery of the Caves imported a Byzantine rule book in 1070 to regulate exactly how monks were supposed to comport themselves without talking. Its status as anyone's working written language finally ended for good in 1708, when Peter the Great rolled out a plainer secular typeface for ordinary Russian business and left the old ornate script to the churches, where congregations still hear it chanted today without necessarily understanding half the words.

#### eras
---
Old Church Slavonic changes with whoever's carrying it: a Cyrillo-Methodian Missionary Era once Cyril and Methodius arrive in Great Moravia, a Bulgarian Golden Age/Literary School Era once Tsar Simeon I makes Preslav its intellectual capital, a Kievan Rus' Christianization Era once Vladimir baptizes Kievan Rus', a Regional Recension Era once distinct Serbian, Russian, and Croatian liturgical traditions solidify, and a Modern Liturgical-Only Era once secular Slavic languages get their own separate typography.
