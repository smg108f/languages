Punjabi is the language whose script was standardized in the 16th century by a Sikh guru specifically so his predecessor's hymns could be written down with one symbol per sound, a level of phonetic precision its users still credit for the script's name: "from the mouth of the Guru." Its spiritual capital was a town founded in 1577 around an excavated pool that a later guru's son would ring with a golden-domed temple, choosing the site less for defense than for the water. Its 19th century ended with a Sikh Empire crushed across four brutal battles in barely four years, and its 20th ended with a 1947 partition that split it in half and killed, by the least generous estimates, hundreds of thousands of its own speakers overnight. Its most recent violent flashpoint was a 1984 military assault on that same golden-domed temple, followed within months by an assassination and days of anti-Sikh massacres that killed thousands more. It still doesn't have a single Punjab-specific school for the Deaf older than 1967, drinks its yogurt thinned into a namesake summer cooler, eats a corn-flour flatbread with a mustard-greens curry that takes all winter to be worth it, and treats kabaddi, a game built entirely around holding your breath while chanting, as a matter of village pride rather than recreation.

#### eras
---
Punjabi changes with whoever's controlling the five rivers: an ancient Shauraseni Prakrit era predating the language's own emergence, a Ghaznavid/Delhi Sultanate era once Lahore falls to Muslim rule, a Sikh Empire era under Maharaja Ranjit Singh, a British Punjab era following annexation, and a post-1947 Partition/Modern Punjab era.
