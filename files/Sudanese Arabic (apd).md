Sudanese Arabic is the language of a land that had its own kingdoms, pyramids, and writing systems for thousands of years before anyone arrived to Arabize it, and then absorbed those arriving Bedouin tribes into its Nubian bloodline so thoroughly that nobody agrees anymore who assimilated whom. It stresses the second-to-last syllable of nearly everything out of what linguists suspect is sheer Nubian stubbornness, considers a glass of hot-pink hibiscus tea a wedding-appropriate beverage, has buried more pyramids in its own sand than Egypt has standing, and has spent the last two decades treating coups, secessions, and civil wars as unwelcome but entirely survivable weather.

#### eras
---
Sudanese Arabic changes with whoever's controlling the Nile valley south of Egypt: ancient Nubian kingdoms whose pyramids and script predate Arabic's arrival by millennia, a centuries-long Arabization that absorbed Bedouin migrants into Nubian and Beja populations rather than the reverse, a Mahdist state that briefly expelled Anglo-Egyptian rule before being crushed at Omdurman, an Anglo-Egyptian condominium that drew the borders of the modern state, and a post-independence era of coups, civil wars, a 2011 secession, and an ongoing conflict that has displaced millions.

global-signers :: ~48,900 (government estimate; considered an undercount)
