Korean is the language of a peninsula whose writing system has the rare distinction of being invented on purpose by a single named king, who then published a manual explaining exactly why every letter is shaped the way it is. It survived 35 years of colonial suppression and a war that split its speakers into two officially still-warring nations sharing one grammar, treats a fermented spicy cabbage side dish as a matter of daily emotional wellbeing, and somehow parlayed a decades-long project of national image rebuilding into an entertainment-export machine that now sells boy bands, subtitled thrillers, and skincare with equal global efficiency.

#### eras
---
Korean changes with whoever's controlling the peninsula: an Old Korean era spanning the Three Kingdoms, a Middle Korean period under Goryeo written mostly in borrowed Chinese characters, a 1443 Hangul era that gave the language a purpose-built native alphabet, a Japanese colonial period that suppressed both the language and its script, a division-and-war era that split the peninsula in two, and a post-war era whose cultural exports eventually became known worldwide as Hallyu.
