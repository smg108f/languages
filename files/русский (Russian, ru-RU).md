Russian is the language of a nation so large it spans eleven time zones, borrowed its alphabet from two Byzantine missionary brothers before its own state had properly formed, and has since produced novels so long they arguably require their own gravitational field to hold together. It treats a shot of chilled vodka as a punctuation mark rather than a beverage, considers a New Year's table more sacred than most religious holidays, survived a Mongol occupation, a Napoleonic invasion, and a war that killed more of its speakers than any conflict in human history, and still found the discipline to produce the most decorated chess players and figure skaters on Earth.

#### eras
---
Russian changes with whoever's controlling the Eastern European plain: a Kievan Rus' era born of Varangian and Slavic fusion, a Mongol-Tatar Yoke that isolated Muscovy from Western Europe for two centuries, a Tsardom that crowned itself an Empire under Peter the Great, a Soviet era that exported the language across a continent-spanning union, and a post-1991 Russian Federation era still working out what comes after that.
