Odia is the language of a coastal kingdom so notoriously bloody to conquer that the emperor who finally did it looked at the corpse count, converted to Buddhism on the spot, and spent the rest of his reign carving apology notes into rock, and it became, in 1936, the first Indian province ever carved out expressly because its people shared a language. It ran its capital out of one riverside city for nearly nine centuries before finally admitting a younger neighbor was more convenient, exported a wooden-chariot festival so globally recognizable that English absorbed its own word for "unstoppable force" directly from it, and still argues with a neighboring state over who invented a syrupy cheese ball dessert, backed by an actual government-certified geographical trademark. It fields one of the country's most decorated national field hockey programs, survived the most intense tropical cyclone ever recorded in its ocean basin, and produced, almost as an afterthought, the twentysomething who built one of the world's largest hotel chains out of a single rented room.

#### eras
---
Odia changes with whoever's controlling Kalinga's ancient coastline: an ancient Kalinga era ended by one of history's bloodiest recorded conquests, a Cuttack-capital era spanning nearly nine centuries, an Eastern Ganga temple-building golden age, a Mughal era after the region's independent Gajapati kingdom finally fell, and a 20th-century statehood era that made Odisha India's first linguistically organized province.
