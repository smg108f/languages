Old Persian is the language of a shepherd-turned-king who founded a new capital at Pasargadae around 546 BCE after toppling the Medes, and then, decades later, had his successor commission a cliff-face inscription so enormous and so far up a rock face that reading it required the same scribes who carved it to have first risked their necks scaling scaffolding to get it there. Its only surviving substantial monument, cut into the cliff at Behistun around 520 BCE, tells the same story three times over in three different cuneiform scripts, a deliberate trilingual redundancy that centuries later let 19th-century scholars use it exactly the way the Rosetta Stone unlocked Egyptian hieroglyphs. Its empire ran fast messages by mounted relay riders who never stopped for weather, and for genuinely urgent news it used something faster still: during the Persian Wars, the general Mardonius flashed word of Athens's capture to King Xerxes across a chain of island beacon-fires, delivering in a single night what a rider would have taken days to carry. Its founder's own clay-cylinder proclamation, discovered in the ruins of Babylon in 1879, freed enslaved and displaced peoples and promised respect for local religions, and gets described today, not entirely without hyperbole, as history's first charter of human rights. Its empire's ceremonial capital at Persepolis, built by that same inscription-commissioning king, went up in flames when Alexander's army arrived in 330 BCE, whether by drunken accident or calculated revenge for the earlier Persian burning of Athens still gets argued about. It still gets read off that same cliff face by specialists working from photographs, since the original site is inconveniently perched somewhere between a highway and a sheer drop.

#### eras
---
Old Persian changes with whoever's ruling from Persis: a Proto-Old-Persian/Migration Era before the Achaemenids consolidate power, an Anshan/Pre-Imperial Era while Persian kings rule a regional kingdom, an Early Achaemenid/Founding Era once Cyrus builds an empire, an Achaemenid Imperial Peak Era once Darius I and Xerxes expand it further, and a Post-Achaemenid/Hellenistic Conquest Era once Alexander ends it.
