Pali is the canonical language of Theravada Buddhism, a Middle Indo-Aryan Prakrit that outlived every kingdom that ever spoke it by becoming the vehicle for the Buddha's teachings instead. Its earliest fixed point is grim: five hundred monks convened at Rajgir around 483 BCE, shortly after the Buddha's death, to recite and codify the Vinaya Pitaka from memory, including the doctrine of Ariya Mona, or Noble Silence, which held that true silence meant stilling body, speech, and mind together rather than merely not talking. Its adoption by Sri Lanka came a couple of centuries later when the monk Mahinda, traditionally said to be Emperor Ashoka's own son, converted King Devanampiya Tissa and founded the Mahavihara monastery at Anuradhapura, turning that city into Theravada Buddhism's institutional home for the next several centuries. Its entire canon survived purely by memorized recitation until a famine around 29 BCE killed enough monks that the survivors panicked and wrote the whole Tipitaka down on palm leaves at the Aluvihara cave complex, a three-year undertaking now remembered as the Fourth Buddhist Council. Its scholarly rediscovery in the West waited until 1881, when T.W. Rhys Davids founded the Pali Text Society in London and started systematically publishing and translating the canon for European academics. Its modern institutional life continues through periodic councils, most recently the Sixth Buddhist Council convened in Yangon between 1954 and 1956, which produced the standardized modern edition of the Tipitaka still used today. Its texts are chanted every day by monks in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, and Laos, and read by meditation students worldwide who couldn't otherwise hold a conversation in it if their retreat depended on it.

#### eras
---
Pali changes with whoever's guarding the canon: an Early Buddhist/Magadhan Era once the First Council codifies the Vinaya at Rajgir, a Sri Lankan Buddhist Era once Mahinda's mission establishes the Mahavihara at Anuradhapura, a Canonical Writing Era once the Fourth Council commits the Tipitaka to palm leaf, a Colonial/Academic Revival Era once Rhys Davids founds the Pali Text Society, and a Modern Theravada/Global Era once the Sixth Buddhist Council standardizes the modern canon.
