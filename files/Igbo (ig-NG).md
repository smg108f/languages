Igbo is the language of a decentralized civilization so allergic to centralized kingship that its most influential precolonial polity ruled mainly through ritual and moral authority rather than armies, spending centuries as a spiritual center other Igbo communities looked to without ever conquering them outright. It once recorded ideas in a secret-society picture script shared with several neighboring peoples, only got its own alphabetic literary tradition from a former slave turned bishop compiling a 17-page primer in 1857, and endured, within living memory, a three-year secessionist war and blockade-induced famine whose death toll estimates still range from the hundreds of thousands to several million. It considers pounded yam and soup a near-sacred combination, keeps palm wine as its ceremonial drink of choice, and effectively founded an entire national film industry when traders in an eastern Nigerian market decided to try their hand at making a movie in 1992.

#### eras
---
Igbo changes with whoever's shaping the Southeast: an ancient Nri era of ritual rather than military authority, a Trans-Atlantic slave trade era that scattered Igbo speakers across the diaspora, a British colonial era of indirect rule and mission-driven literacy, a Biafran secession era defined by civil war and famine, and a post-1970 reintegration era within a reunified Nigeria.
