Talossan is the official language of a nation that began as a teenager's bedroom. On December 26, 1979, 14-year-old Robert Ben Madison, grieving the recent death of his mother, declared that his second-floor bedroom in a house on Milwaukee's east side had seceded from the United States as the Kingdom of Talossa, taking its name from the Finnish word for "inside the house." The following year, at age 15, Madison created the Talossan language itself, building it primarily from Occitan and Rhaeto-Romance roots with substantial borrowings from English, other Romance languages, Berber languages, Native American languages, and Celtic languages; he grew its vocabulary through Støtanneu, a mimeographed newspaper he issued entirely in Talossan throughout the 1980s. In 1983, Madison formed the Comità per l'Útzil del Glheþ (CÚG, the Committee for the Use of the Language) to formally govern the language's ongoing development, and by the mid-1990s Talossa had established a continuous web presence, helping inspire a wave of later internet micronations. Talossa underwent a serious political schism in 2004, when citizens in four of its seven provinces broke away to form the rival Republic of Talossa, accusing Madison of autocratic rule; Madison abdicated in 2005, and the two governments gradually reconciled, with the Republic formally dissolving back into the Kingdom on April 20, 2012 to found the new Province of Fiôvâ. Because Talossan has no gestural tradition of its own, and the Kingdom itself is rooted in Wisconsin, it borrows a thematic analog from that state's deaf-education history: the Wisconsin School for the Deaf, founded in Delavan on April 19, 1852.
