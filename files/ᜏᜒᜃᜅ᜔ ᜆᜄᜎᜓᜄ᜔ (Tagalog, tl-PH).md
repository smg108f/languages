Tagalog is the language of a river-mouth settlement whose name literally means "people from the river," and whose earliest surviving written record is a copper plate from the year 900 forgiving someone's debt. It kept its own pre-colonial syllabic script alive for centuries, only got its first printed book once Spanish friars needed to preach in it, and then spent the next three hundred years watching its home city serve first as a Spanish viceregal capital, then as the site of one of the deadliest urban battles of the entire Pacific War. It fought a war of independence against Spain, then almost immediately another one against the country that had promised to help it get that independence, endured a two-decade dictatorship whose human rights toll is still being tallied, and somehow still finds room to obsess, nearly year-round, over noontime variety shows, basketball, and a national dish that's really just several regional dishes wearing the same name.

#### eras
---
Tagalog changes with whoever's controlling Manila Bay: an ancient Tondo/Maynila era attested by the region's earliest written record, a Spanish colonial era launched when Manila became the viceregal capital, an American colonial era opened by a war fought against the country that had just liberated it from Spain, a Japanese Occupation era during WWII, and a post-1946 independence era including two decades of martial law.
