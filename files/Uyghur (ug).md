Uyghur is the language of a steppe empire that collapsed so completely in 840 that its surviving population fled south and rebuilt an entirely different kind of kingdom around oasis towns instead, one whose rulers eventually converted to Islam after a khan's decision in 934 sent an entire dynasty of Turkic tribes toward the same religion within a generation. Its scholars compiled the first great Turkic dictionary in Kashgar in the 1070s, cataloguing an entire language family for an audience that had never had one written down for them before. Its oasis kingdoms were conquered, reconquered, and renamed by a rotating cast of Mongol, Dzungar, and Qing armies over the following thousand years, with the final Qing conquest in 1759 alone killing an estimated several hundred thousand people across the wider region. Its more recent history includes a policy of mass internment that international human rights organizations and multiple governments estimate has detained over a million Uyghurs and other Turkic Muslims since 2017, a characterization the Chinese government disputes. It still bakes its bread in a tandoor-style oven, ferments its grapes into a home-brewed wine, and sends men walking across canyon gorges on tightropes strung between cliffs, a performance tradition old enough that nobody remembers who started it.

#### eras
---
Uyghur changes with whoever's controlling the Tarim Basin: an ancient Kingdom of Qocho era following the collapse of the Uyghur Khaganate, a Karakhanid Islamic era beginning with Satuq Bughra Khan's conversion, a Qing Conquest era after the fall of the Dzungar Khanate, a Qing Xinjiang Province era, and a post-1955 Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region era.
