Madurese is the language of an island whose most famous export isn't a person or a product but a personal-honor dueling custom so notorious it gets whispered about across the rest of Indonesia, alongside a bull-racing sport that turns farm animals into 130-meter sprinters accompanied by a double-reed instrument loud enough to be heard over hoofbeats. It grew out of a 13th-century exile's appointment as a Javanese-backed regional governor, later produced a prince whose own rebellion briefly captured the Mataram Sultanate's royal capital, and settled for centuries into writing itself in borrowed Javanese and Arabic-derived scripts before switching to the Latin alphabet like everyone else. It ships its trader diaspora into nearly every Indonesian province going, occasionally to violent local resentment, cooks a peanut-sauce satay so popular it's simply become "the" satay nationwide, and still keeps a nine-form devotional singing tradition alive that most of its own younger speakers have never heard performed live.

#### eras
---
Madurese changes with whoever's controlling the island's four regencies: an ancient petty-states era before unification, a Sumenep Duchy era launched by a Singhasari-appointed governor, a Mataram-vassal era of shifting central-Javanese overlordship, a colonial Dutch East Indies era, and a post-1945 Indonesian era including the diaspora-driven Sampit conflict.
