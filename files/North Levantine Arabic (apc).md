North Levantine Arabic is the language of a coastline that has been conquered so many times it started treating occupation as a seasoning rather than a catastrophe, absorbing Aramaic, Turkish, and French into its vocabulary the way its kitchens absorb whatever herbs happen to be in season. It considers a plate of parsley to be a grain dish rather than a garnish, keeps its capital cities' rulers on record since the 10th century while everyone around them changed empires every few generations, insists that morning coffee and evening arak both count as essential food groups, and has spent the last half-century proving that a civil war, a foreign occupation, and a currency collapse are all things a good dinner party can survive.

#### eras
---
North Levantine Arabic changes with whichever empire or militia controls Damascus, Aleppo, and Beirut at a given moment: an Aramaic-speaking pre-Islamic Levant absorbed into the early caliphate, an Umayyad capital that briefly ruled from Damascus over an empire stretching to Iberia, a French Mandate that carved Lebanon out of Greater Syria and left behind a permanent taste for French loanwords, a 15-year civil war that killed over 100,000 people and displaced a million more, and a contemporary diaspora era scattering Syrian and Lebanese speakers worldwide following a currency collapse and a decade of civil war.
