Bulgarian is the language of an empire founded in 681 by a Bulgar khan who defeated a Byzantine army and set up his capital in a fortified camp, one that outlasted that same Byzantine Empire's attempts to reclaim it for over two centuries. It got its first literary school not from its own capital but from a satellite city its own ruler moved the government to in 893, specifically to make room for a monk-run workshop developing the Cyrillic alphabet that would eventually spread across the Slavic world. It rose up against its Ottoman rulers in 1876 in a rebellion so brutally crushed that outside observers put the civilian death toll as high as 40,000, and the international outrage that followed did more to win it independence than the uprising itself. It won a war against the empire that had ruled it for five centuries, then immediately lost most of the spoils fighting its own recent allies over the leftovers a few months later. It ferments milk with a bacterium that carries its own country's name, distills fruit into a brandy served before the meal instead of after, and danced across hot coals as a sacred ritual long before anyone thought to write any of it down.

#### eras
---
Bulgarian changes with whoever's controlling the lower Danube: an ancient First Bulgarian Empire era launched by Khan Asparuh's founding of Pliska, an Ottoman era following the fall of the Second Bulgarian Empire, a Third Bulgarian State era after the 1878 liberation, a People's Republic era under communist rule, and a post-1989 democratic Republic era.
