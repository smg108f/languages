Argentine Spanish is the cousin who moved to the port city, took Italian singing lessons, and never gave back the "tú" it borrowed from the rest of the family, replacing it with "vos" out of sheer regional pride. It punctuates nearly every sentence with "che" as if constantly getting someone's attention, pronounces its double-L and Y as a soft "sh" that unsettles the rest of the Spanish-speaking world, and built an entire national identity around tango, psychoanalysis, and explaining at length exactly why its football is better than everyone else's.

#### eras
---
Argentine Spanish changes with each wave of ships into Buenos Aires harbor and each government that follows: a colonial outpost, an independent republic, a country remade by European immigration, a populist upheaval, a military dictatorship, and now a restored democracy narrating itself back to the world in telenovelas and football commentary.
