Interslavic exists to let speakers of Slavic languages understand each other without either side learning the other's tongue outright, and it reached that goal by merging several competing projects rather than starting from nothing. In March 2006, a group including Ondrej Rečnik, Gabriel Svoboda, Jan van Steenbergen, Igor Polyakov, Vojtěch Merunka, and Steeven Radzikowski launched Slovianski, itself created partly as a response to the more artificial-feeling Slovio; Slovianski split into naturalistic and simplified variants before, in 2011, it merged with the related Slovioski and Novoslověnsky projects into a single, unified language under the name Interslavic (Medžuslovjanski), built to draw from prototypes shared across the Slavic family rather than fixed rules from any one branch. The language reached its first official, public milestone on June 1-2, 2017, when the inaugural Conference on Interslavic Language (CISLa) convened in Staré Město near Uherské Hradiště, Czech Republic, drawing 64 participants from 12 countries. Two years later, Czech director Václav Marhoul used Interslavic, developed in large part by Czech academic Vojtěch Merunka, as the primary dialogue language of The Painted Bird, a nearly three-hour World War II drama that premiered at the Venice Film Festival on September 3, 2019 and went on to sweep the 2019 Czech Lion Awards with nine wins including Best Film; Marhoul chose the invented language specifically so no single Slavic nation would feel the story was about them. Because Interslavic itself has no gestural tradition of its own, and its development has been centered substantially in the Czech Republic, it borrows a thematic analog from that country's deaf history: the Czech Sign Language Act, passed on June 11, 1998.
