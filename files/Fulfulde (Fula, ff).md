Fulfulde is the language of history's largest nomadic pastoral community, a population spread across some twenty countries with no single capital to call its own, until an 18th-century Islamic scholar declared holy war on the Hausa kings around him and his son built one from scratch in 1809. Its literature was written for centuries in a modified Arabic script borrowed from the Quran, until two boys, aged ten and fourteen, invented an entirely new alphabet with their eyes closed in 1989 specifically so their language would stop disappearing into everyone else's writing systems. Its jihad-era empire briefly ruled ten to twenty million people across a swath of West Africa nearly a thousand miles wide, and its cattle-herding descendants are still fighting, this time with farmers over land and water rather than kings over religion, in conflicts that have killed thousands across Nigeria's central states over the past two decades. Its young men prove their readiness for marriage by standing still while rivals whip them with sticks, and young men from one subgroup paint their faces and dance for days so a panel of women can judge which of them is the most beautiful.

#### eras
---
Fulfulde changes with whoever's controlling the Sahel: an ancient Fulɓe pastoralist migration era spreading eastward across West Africa, a Futa Jallon jihad-state era founding the first Fulani Islamic theocracy, a Sokoto Caliphate/Fulani jihad era under Usman dan Fodio, a colonial era following European conquest of the jihad states, and a post-1960 independent nation-states era across the many countries Fulani communities now inhabit.
