Mesopotamian Arabic is the language of a floodplain that invented writing itself and has spent every millennium since making sure nobody forgets it, sitting on the same two rivers that produced the first epic poem, the first law code, and a bureaucratic streak so old it predates the alphabet it eventually adopted. It splits its own speakers into two camps over how one pronounces the word for "I said," treats a wood-grilled river carp as a dish worth naming a cuisine after, and has spent the last half-century proving that wars, sanctions, and occupations can pile up one after another without managing to make the local tea any less sweet or any less mandatory.

#### eras
---
Mesopotamian Arabic changes with whoever's ruling the land between the Tigris and Euphrates: an ancient Sumerian-Akkadian-Assyrian-Babylonian run of civilizations that invented writing and law before Arabic ever arrived, an Abbasid caliphate that made Baghdad the intellectual capital of the medieval world, a centuries-long Ottoman administration, a 20th-century monarchy and republic that discovered oil and coups in roughly equal measure, and a contemporary post-invasion era rebuilding from war, sanctions, and the fight against ISIS.
