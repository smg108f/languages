Cantonese is the older sibling who never got over losing the family throne, maintaining that its nine tones preserve the true sound of ancient Chinese poetry while Mandarin flattened everything down to four out of sheer laziness. It exported kung fu, dim sum, and a small army of gangster films to the entire planet through a single overcrowded harbor city, insists a phrase isn't properly emphatic until it's been sworn in at least three overlapping tones, and has spent the last few decades watching a national government quietly try to replace it in its own hometown.

#### eras
---
Cantonese changes with whoever's controlling the Pearl River Delta and how open Canton is to the outside world: an ancient Yue kingdom absorbed into the Chinese empire, a brief independent kingdom centered on Guangzhou, a Qing-era trading system that funneled all of China's foreign commerce through a single port, a British colony grafted onto that port's overflow, and now a global media era broadcasting Cantopop and kung fu films to a diaspora spread across the world.
