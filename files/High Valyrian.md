High Valyrian is Dothraki's more grammatically elaborate sibling, built by the same linguist for the same television show but designed to sound like a dead classical language rather than a living nomadic one. David J. Peterson, who had already won HBO's 2009 competition to build Dothraki for Game of Thrones, developed High Valyrian's declensions, noun cases, and vocabulary in 2012 to give the show's Essos-set storylines an imagined ancient tongue, modeling its role on real-world classical languages like Latin and Greek even as its sounds and grammar remained entirely his own invention. The language reached television audiences on March 31, 2013, when Game of Thrones' Season 3 premiere, "Valar Dohaeris," gave the show's first extended High Valyrian dialogue to Emilia Clarke as Daenerys Targaryen and Nathalie Emmanuel as Missandei; later High Valyrian-heavy scenes were filmed across Osuna, Spain, Dubrovnik, Croatia, and Ouarzazate, Morocco. High Valyrian's biggest leap beyond the show came on July 12, 2017, when Duolingo launched a full beta course teaching it, built in collaboration with Peterson; the course has since drawn well over a million learners. Because High Valyrian itself has no gestural tradition, and Duolingo is headquartered in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, it borrows a thematic analog from that city's own deaf history: the Western Pennsylvania School for the Deaf, founded in 1869 as the nation's first day school for deaf children.
