Sindarin is the Welsh-flavored cousin of Quenya, and like Quenya it began as a private notebook project rather than a planned publication. J.R.R. Tolkien compiled the "Gnomish Lexicon" (I·Lam na·Ngoldathon) in 1917, an early wordlist for what he then called Gnomish or Goldogrin; over the following decades he reworked its grammar into a language called Noldorin, and, in October 1920, took up a Readership in English Language at the University of Leeds, developing the linguistic side of its School of English Studies before his 1925 move to Oxford. Around 1944, Tolkien restructured Noldorin once more, blending it with material from an earlier tongue called Ilkorin Doriathrin and giving the result a distinctly Welsh-inspired phonology and set of consonant mutations; he renamed the finished language Sindarin, the tongue of the Grey-elves, in time for its role as the everyday Elvish of The Lord of the Rings. Decades later, Peter Jackson's Hobbit trilogy (2012-2014), filmed largely around Wellington, New Zealand, gave Sindarin its biggest on-screen expansion yet, with entire scenes among Thranduil's Woodland Realm performed in the language. Because Sindarin itself has no gestural tradition, and Tolkien built its sound on Welsh, it borrows a thematic analog from Welsh deaf history instead: the founding, at an 1847 public meeting in Aberystwyth, of the Cambrian Institution for the Deaf and Dumb, later relocated to Swansea.
