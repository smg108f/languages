Oromo is the language of history's most understudied democracy: a cyclical eight-year power-rotation system whose exact founding nobody can date, which was busy governing large stretches of the Horn of Africa long before the empire that eventually absorbed it built its own capital in 1886. That empire's expansion into Oromo territory produced, within a single year, a battle that killed more than 12,000 people and a follow-up incident in which soldiers amputated the right hand and right breast of over a thousand men and women, a punishment specifically designed to prevent anyone involved from ever picking up a weapon or nursing a child again. Its language went unwritten for so long that the first complete Bible translation, finished in 1899, functioned for nearly a century as one of the only substantial texts anyone could point to, until a convention of more than a thousand Oromo intellectuals voted in 1991 to formally adopt a Latin alphabet nobody's grandparents had been allowed to use in school. Its most beloved modern protest singer built a career on turning a folk melody into an anthem about being pushed out of the capital his own ancestors named, and was shot dead in 2020, a killing that set off unrest the government is still working through. It drinks its coffee in three ceremonial rounds with popcorn and frankincense smoke, eats barley porridge with a spoon carved from a cattle horn, and races horses at festivals timed to a calendar system most of the world has never heard of.

#### eras
---
Oromo changes with whoever's controlling the highlands: an ancient Gadaa System era of indigenous cyclical democratic governance, a Great Oromo Migration era of population expansion across the Horn, an Ethiopian Empire Incorporation era following Menelik II's conquest, a Derg/Socialist Ethiopia era, and a post-1991 Federal Oromia era following the fall of the Derg.
