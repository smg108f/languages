Marathi is the language of a warrior state that clawed an empire out from under the Mughals one hill fort at a time, kept two parallel scripts running for centuries so that administrators could write faster than the poets, and lost an entire army in a single January afternoon 700 miles from home before rebuilding itself into modern India's financial and industrial capital. It treats a deep-fried spiced potato fritter jammed into a bread roll as a complete meal and a matter of civic pride, considers a saint-poet's 13th-century commentary on the Bhagavad Gita to be the true founding document of its literature, and still can't quite decide whether that empire's ultimate legacy was a kingdom or a stock exchange.

#### eras
---
Marathi changes with whoever's controlling the Deccan plateau: an early Prakrit-descended era predating any fixed "Marathi" identity, a Yadava-dynasty literary flowering that produced the language's oldest surviving major text, a Deccan Sultanate interval, a 17th-century Maratha Empire that expanded Marathi's political reach across most of the subcontinent, a British colonial period, and a 1960 linguistic-reorganization movement that carved out a dedicated Marathi-speaking state.
