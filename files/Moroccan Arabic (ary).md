Moroccan Arabic is the language of a kingdom perched on Africa's northwest corner that decided the correct way to speak was to borrow from everyone who ever passed through, folding Berber grammar, Spanish loanwords, and French vocabulary into a single sentence without pausing to apologize to any of the source languages. It considers a shared pot of mint tea a more binding social contract than most written agreements, kept a university running in continuous operation since before most of Europe's oldest institutions existed, treats couscous on Friday as a near-legal obligation rather than a suggestion, and has spent the last century proving that a protectorate, a war in the mountains, and independence can all be absorbed into the dialect as casually as another loanword.

#### eras
---
Darija changes with whichever dynasty or protectorate controls Fez, Marrakech, and Rabat: an Amazigh (Berber) pre-Islamic substrate absorbed into an early Islamic conquest, an Idrisid and later Almoravid/Almohad run of empires that made Morocco a Mediterranean and trans-Saharan power, a Marinid and Alaouite dynastic run that carried Morocco into the modern era largely unconquered by the Ottomans, a French and Spanish protectorate era that layered European vocabulary onto the dialect within living memory, and a post-independence era exporting Gnawa music and Moroccan cinema to festival circuits worldwide.
