Mongolian is the language of a warlord who, having eliminated every rival tribe on the steppe by 1206, commissioned a captured Uyghur scribe to adapt his people's alphabet to a language that had never been written down before. Its first real capital was a yurt town on the Orkhon River that Genghis Khan founded in 1220 and that his son later walled and filled with a palace specifically so the empire he was building would have somewhere permanent to receive foreign envoys. Its 13th-century conquests are estimated, across sources that disagree wildly, to have killed somewhere between 30 and 40 million people, a death toll driven in part by a stated Mongol policy of executing a fixed quota of captives per soldier in cities that resisted. Its 20th century included an 18-month Soviet-directed purge that executed as many as 35,000 people, nearly all of the country's Buddhist monastic class among them, and flattened over 700 monasteries in the process. It didn't get an organized school for its Deaf community until 1964, built with Soviet assistance and so closely modeled on Russian Sign Language that the two only fully diverged afterward. It still drinks fermented mare's milk at its national summer festival, eats dumplings filled with mutton fat, and settles national bragging rights every July through wrestling, archery, and cross-country horse races run by riders as young as five.

#### eras
---
Mongolian changes with whoever's controlling the steppe: an ancient Mongol-tribal era predating Genghis Khan's unification, a Mongol Empire era once Temüjin becomes Genghis Khan, a Yuan/Northern Yuan Dynasty era of Chinese and post-Chinese rule, a Qing Rule era once Mongolia is absorbed into the Manchu empire, and a post-1921 Modern Mongolia era beginning with the 1921 Revolution.
