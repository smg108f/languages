Burmese is the language of a dry-zone kingdom that once packed more than two thousand brick temples into a single riverside plain, largely as an ongoing multi-generational competition in merit-making, and whose unifying 11th-century king is still credited as the father of the whole modern nation. It wrote itself in a script so persistently circular that centuries of scribes blamed the shape entirely on palm leaves splitting under straight lines, survived six decades of intermittent war against a colonial power that eventually annexed the entire country anyway, and then built itself, in the 21st century, a brand-new capital city from scratch in the middle of nowhere for reasons the government has never fully explained. It keeps a national dish built from fish, rice noodles, and whatever crispy things happen to be on hand that morning, fields boxers who fight bare-knuckle with fists, elbows, knees, and headbutts all in the same bout, and continues, as of the most recent counts, to be fighting one of the world's deadliest active civil wars.

#### eras
---
Burmese changes with whoever's controlling the Irrawaddy Valley: a Pagan Empire era launched by Anawrahta's unification of the valley and adoption of Theravada Buddhism, a Later Kingdoms era of successor capitals after Pagan's fall, a colonial era opened by six decades of Anglo-Burmese wars, a post-independence era of shifting civilian and military rule, and a current era defined by a 2021 coup and the civil war it triggered.
