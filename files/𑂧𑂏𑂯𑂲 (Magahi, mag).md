Magahi is the language of the empire that gave the ancient world one of its most consequential rulers, a hilltop capital fortified by a 15-year-old king in 544 BCE, and, a couple of centuries later, an emperor so shaken by the carnage of his own conquest that he renounced war outright and carved the apology into stone edicts still readable today. It produced its earliest known literature from tantric Buddhist masters teaching at Nalanda around the 8th century, watched its own trading-script cousin get replaced by Devanagari during the colonial period, and didn't get its first surviving novel until 1928, written specifically to stop the language from being, in its own author's words, "gobbled up" by Hindi. It stuffs spiced roasted-gram flour into little wheat-dough balls and calls the result a national comfort food, keeps a moonlit courtship folk-dance alive about two ill-fated lovers and a wicked boatman, and got the very first sound film in its own language only in 1961.

#### eras
---
Magahi changes with whoever's controlling the Magadha heartland: an ancient Magadha Mahajanapada era launched by a hilltop capital, a Mauryan Empire era of imperial expansion under Ashoka, a Gupta-era classical revival, a Delhi Sultanate/Mughal era of shifting imperial control, and a post-1947 Indian statehood era within Bihar.
