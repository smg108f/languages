Haitian Creole is the language of the only nation in history founded by a successful slave revolt, one that declared independence in 1804 after thirteen years of war that killed, by some estimates, half a million people on both sides, including nearly the entire French expeditionary force Napoleon sent to reconquer it. Its capital had already been designated thirty-four years earlier by the same colonial power it would go on to defeat, a French port city its new Black government simply kept using rather than build something else from scratch. Its literature stayed almost entirely oral for a century and a half after independence, dismissed by the country's own French-educated elite as a peasant patois unfit for print, until a 20th-century poet spent years lobbying to get it recognized as an official language and a national spelling reform in 1979 finally gave it one standardized way to be written down. It's currently home to a spiraling gang-violence crisis that killed more than 5,600 people in 2024 alone, according to the United Nations. It eats squash soup on every January 1st to commemorate a freedom its enslaved ancestors were once forbidden from tasting, distills its sugarcane into a rum aged in oak like French cognac, and treats a game of dominoes, played loudly and competitively on street corners, as a near-sacred national pastime.

#### eras
---
Haitian Creole changes with whoever's controlling Hispaniola: an ancient Taíno era predating European contact, a Spanish colonial era following Columbus's arrival, a French colonial era after the Treaty of Ryswick, a Haitian Revolution era, and a post-1804 Independent Republic era.
