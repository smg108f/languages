Toki Pona was built to simplify one person's mind rather than to unite nations or decorate a franchise. Canadian translator and polyglot Sonja Lang, who grew up bilingual in New Brunswick and now lives in Toronto, first published the language online in 2001 while working through a period of depression, deliberately paring her invented vocabulary down to just over a hundred root words and a handful of grammar rules as a way of reconstructing her own thinking in simpler terms. The project stayed a personal, evolving sketch for over a decade until Lang published Toki Pona: The Language of Good in May 2014, which fixed the language's vocabulary at 120 words and unveiled sitelen pona, her pictographic logography for writing it. Growth accelerated once the community moved onto Discord, where the "ma pona pi toki pona" server, active by 2018 and numbering over 19,000 members as of February 2026, became the language's real-time meeting ground; Lang drew on the server's volunteers to compile the Toki Pona Dictionary, published in 2021 with more than 11,000 entries. That same year, the community-built Luka Pona ("good hand") sign language emerged, created by jan Olipija and drawing on the grammar of natural sign languages like ASL, BSL, and Plains Indian Sign Language rather than simply transliterating Toki Pona word-for-word. After two earlier failed applications, Toki Pona finally received the ISO 639-3 language code "tok" from SIL International on January 20, 2022.
