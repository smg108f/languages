Tatar is the language of a khanate founded in 1438 by a deposed ruler of the Golden Horde who simply set up shop in a city built on the ruins of a Volga trading empire the Mongols had already devastated two centuries earlier. Its first great literary work, a 1233 verse retelling of the Qur'anic story of Joseph, was copied by hand into more than 200 surviving manuscripts before it was ever printed, and outlived the medieval kingdom that produced it by more than five centuries. Its capital fell in 1552 to a Russian army whose siege guns leveled its walls, after which imperial missionaries spent the next two centuries tearing down mosques and pressuring Tatars to convert. Its script has changed three times in the last century alone, from Arabic to a briefly used Latin alphabet to the Cyrillic one it uses today, each switch handed down by a government in Moscow rather than voted on locally. It fries dough into a honey-soaked dessert served at every celebration, wrestles by the belt at a summer festival held every year since long before anyone wrote the rules down, and still argues, mildly, about whether its wealthiest family's petrochemical fortune counts as a family business or a state one.

#### eras
---
Tatar changes with whoever's controlling the Volga: an ancient Volga Bulgaria Islamic era beginning with the Bulgars' conversion, a Kazan Khanate era founded on the ruins of that state, a Russian Imperial Incorporation era after the fall of Kazan, a Tatar ASSR/Soviet era, and a post-1991 Republic of Tatarstan era.
