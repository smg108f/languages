Brazilian Portuguese is the child who left home, discovered the beach, and never looked back, now outnumbering its parent by more than twenty to one while insisting its softer, more melodic pronunciation is simply the language relaxing into its best self. It nasalizes vowels the old country never dreamed of, swapped the formal "tu" for the friendlier "você" as if the whole nation collectively decided formality wasn't worth the effort, and exports telenovelas, samba, and a national holiday's worth of Carnival choreography to a planet that still can't quite get the hip movement right.

#### eras
---
Brazilian Portuguese changes with whoever's running Brasília and how the country is redefining itself against its colonial past: a plantation colony harvesting dyewood and sugar, an empire, a republic, a populist strongman's modernization project, a military dictatorship, and now a redemocratized giant broadcasting telenovelas and funk to the rest of the Portuguese-speaking world.
